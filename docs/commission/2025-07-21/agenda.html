RELATED TO RAPID REHOUSING AND SUPPORTIVE SERVICES;

PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

Background Information

The City, in partnership with Palm Beach County’s Division of Human Services and Community Action (HSCA), applied for federal funding through the FY 2022 Continuum of Care (CoC) Program Competition.

HSCA oversees the annual U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) CoC competition and is the Collaborative Applicant for the Palm Beach County Homeless and Housing Alliance (HHA), the local CoC. The City of West Palm Beach will serve as a project subrecipient with the Housing and Community Development Department (HCD) responsible for administering the project.

The proposed project, The Pathway to Independence Program (the “Program), was awarded $532,445 to provide housing assistance and supportive services, including rental assistance, utility deposits, and case management, to people experiencing homelessness. The City was required to provide an in-kind match of $133,112; which is supported through staff time spent on program implementation. The Program is based on the Housing First rapid re-housing model. It will assist up to twenty-two (22) chronically homeless and medically fragile individuals who are unsheltered and have a desire to be placed into permanent housing. Program participants will reside within the municipal boundaries of the City and will be identified based on the partnerships with street outreach teams through the Coordinated Entry System in Palm Beach County.

The Program is based upon a declining subsidy model and will provide 100% of the first, last, security deposit, and an additional three (3) months at 100% subsidy. The subsidy will be decreased every three (3) months by 25%, dependent on the financial assessment of the program participant. Subsidy reduction will be at the discretion of City staff. The programmatic goal is to have each client linked to other supportive services and maintain self-sufficiency without subsidy within twelve (12) months. The Program may last up to twelve (12) months for persons who may need such. Rent and deposit payments will be made directly to landlords and utility providers utilizing Palm Beach County’s Online System for Community Access to Resources and Social Services

(OSCARSS).

The Program demonstrates the City’s continued commitment to addressing unsheltered homelessness through a collaborative approach

City Commission - Jul 21 2025

with Palm Beach County and other service providers that will assist in providing supportive services.

Eligible supportive services costs include case management, employment assistance, outpatient health services, damage deposit, utility deposit assistance, food assistance, transportation assistance, and operating assistance, including a percentage of staff time.

The City must submit reimbursement requests for the supportive services costs through the Services and Activities Management Information System (SAMIS). The supportive services line item budget is as follows:
- Transportation Assistance: $2,000 Mental Health Services: $36,000 Case Management/Staff Time: $192,909 Supportive Services Grand Total: $230,909

Resolution No. 119-25 authorizes the Mayor to execute the interlocal agreement and related documents necessary to implement the Program.

Resolution No. 175-25(F) authorizes the appropriation of HUD CoC proceeds in the Grant Programs Special Revenue Fund for the Pathways to Independence Program.

Originating Department

Ordinance/Resolution

RESOLUTION NO. 157-25: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, APPROVING PARTICIPATION IN THE DEPARTMENT OF ENERGY BETTER CLIMATE CHALLENGE; AUTHORIZING THE MAYOR TO EXECUTE A PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

Background Information

The Better Climate Challenge is a national leadership initiative calling on chief executive officers, university presidents, and state and local leaders to reduce Greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, create jobs, and promote healthy, safe, and thriving communities.

City Commission - Jul 21 2025

In 2012, the City of West Palm Beach (City) established a partnership with the Department of Energy (DOE) when it signed an agreement approving participation in the Better Building Challenge (Resolution No. 104-12). At that time, the goal of the Better Building Challenge was: “to make American buildings 20 percent more energy efficient by 2020 to reduce national dependence on foreign oil, protect the environment, and save billions of dollars in energy cost, investing in new technologies, and creating American jobs.” Since participating in the Better Building Challenge, the City has achieved several energy-efficiency goals: 1. A 20% reduction in energy intensity by 2022, from the 2010 baseline, was achieved in 2014. 2. A 15% reduction in energy intensity by 2025, from the 2015 baseline, was surpassed in 2023 with a 16% reduction across buildings and the plant portfolio.

By joining the Better Climate Challenge and signing the Partnership Agreement, the City of West Palm Beach would further enhance its commitment to energy efficiency and reduction of GHG emissions. The
City would agree to the following:
1. Publicly pledge to reduce municipal GHG emissions by 63% across the building and plant portfolio by 2030, from the 2018 baseline, without the use of offsets. This goal aligns with the 2030 Science Base Target and Net Zero by 2050 goal for emission reduction. 2. Continue the Better Building Challenge initiative with a new municipal energy-efficiency target of a 25% reduction in energy use over twelve (12) years from the 2018 baseline, as part of the decarbonization strategy. 3. Develop an organization-wide plan with GHG reduction milestones. 4. Share information on the low GHG strategies implemented to achieve the GHG reduction commitment. 5. Participate in at least one (1) working group to discuss barriers, exchange best practices, and identify solutions. 6. Assign a senior executive and primary point of contact to work closely with DOE.

To achieve these goals, collaboration across departments will be enhanced through the Climate Resilience and Infrastructure Committee and the Office of Sustainability. Efforts will be made to leverage funding opportunities such as the elective pay tax incentives available via the

City Commission - Jul 21 2025

Inflation Reduction Act and various State and Federal grant opportunities and programs.

Resolution No. 157-25 commits to participation in the Challenge, agrees to the new energy goals and authorizes the Mayor to sign the Partnership Agreement.

7.5. Resolution No. 173-25 approving an Interlocal Agreement with Palm Beach County for renewal of the six-cent fuel tax.

Originating Department

Engineering

Ordinance/Resolution

RESOLUTION NO. 173-25: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, CONSENTING TO THE RE-DETERMINATION, RE-ADOPTION AND RATIFICATION OF THE EXISTING DISTRIBUTION FORMULA FOR THE SIX-CENT LOCAL OPTION FUEL TAX; APPROVING AN INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH AND PALM BEACH COUNTY; PROVIDING FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND OTHER PURPOSES.

Background Information

In 1995, Palm Beach County re-levied a six-cent local option fuel tax for a 30-year term in accordance with Florida Statutes and County Ordinance.

Palm Beach County established an interlocal agreement with various municipalities representing a majority of the population of the incorporated area within the County, the distribution formula for dividing the entire proceeds of the fuel tax among the County and all eligible municipalities within the County.

The 1995 County Ordinance and the Interlocal Agreements with the municipalities will terminate on August 31, 2025, in accordance with the terms prescribed therein and with the Florida Statute.

The County desires to once again re-levy and re-impose the fuel tax for a new 30-year term to expire on August 31, 2055, pursuant to the Florida Statute, and enter into Interlocal Agreements with all eligible municipalities representing a majority of population of the incorporated area within the County to re-determine the distribution formula for dividing the entire proceeds of the fuel tax among the County and the municipalities, including the City of West Palm Beach.

City Commission - Jul 21 2025

This Interlocal Agreement re-adopts and ratifies the distribution formula currently in effect for dividing the entire proceeds of the fuel tax among the County and all eligible municipalities within the County. The same formula for determining the municipality distributions that was used in 1995 will be in effect for this new 30-year period.

On June 3, 2025, the Palm Beach County Board of Commissioners enacted Ordinance No. 2025-013, re-imposing the Six Cent Local Option Fuel Tax for distribution among the County and eligible municipalities.

The County receives 66.5648% of this tax revenue, and municipalities collectively share the remaining 33.4352%, pursuant to the distribution formula based upon population and roadway lane miles. Of the municipalities collective share, thirty percent (30%) will be distributed to the participating municipalities as calculated by the ratio of the municipality’s population to the sum of the population of all the municipalities in the County, and seventy percent (70) of it will be the ratio of the municipality’s total lane miles to the sum of all the lane miles in the municipalities in the County.

The total revenue the City of West Palm Beach received from this program in Fiscal Year 2025 was $2,937,058.

The City allocates these revenues in accordance with the Fuel Tax Statute restrictions for transportation costs, including public transportation operations and maintenance and capital projects, including construction or reconstruction of roadways and sidewalks.

Resolution No. 173-25 will authorize the Mayor to sign the interlocal Agreement for execution by Palm Beach County.

Fiscal Note

In FY 2025, the City received $2,937,058 in fuel tax revenue. There is no cost to the City for receiving such revenue.

Ordinance/Resolution

BEACH, FLORIDA, FOR THE PURPOSE OF AMENDING THE FLEET

FUNDING FROM RESERVES FOR THE PURCHASE OF THE POLICE

DEPARTMENT MOBILE COMMAND CENTER; PROVIDING AN

EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

Background Information

The West Palm Beach Police Department (WPBPD) is currently replacing the Police Department Mobile Command Vehicle/Center (MCV).

Currently, the WPBPD operates a 2004-manufactured Ford chassis Winnebago, which is considered end of service life. The current MCV can no longer fulfill the requirements needed when utilized under the nationally recognized Incident Command System (ICS) operational model or support the technological advancements of modern policing in conjunction with the WPBPD Strategic Intelligence Center (STIC). The current MCV is not compatible with retrofitting requirements.

The MCV will serve as a unified command hub for all major incidents, including but not limited to (e.g., special events, public demonstrations, natural disasters, weapons of mass destruction, hostage rescue, major crime scenes, and critical incidents). The MCV will enable coordination with all federal and local response units under the mutual aid agreements and serve as a technological hub for intelligence sources and interoperable radio systems. A modern MCV will provide the WPBPD with the ability to rapidly deploy to any location where the communication infrastructure is inadequate or has been disrupted, or where enhanced interoperability among law enforcement and fire/rescue agencies is needed.

Incident management requires mobility, interoperability among public safety agencies, reliability, and cost effectiveness. In the event the WPBPD and WPBFR need to conduct public safety operations without access and/or use of police/fire/rescue facilities, the MCV can serve as a temporary operations hub, including an operational dispatch center. A modern MCV can further enhance public safety services when working in tandem with a communications Site on Wheels (SOW). The MCV and SOW allow for the continuation of uninterrupted public safety services, especially during and post-natural disaster incidents, of which South Florida is at high risk of experiencing. The MCV will also provide an environmentally controlled platform for on-scene command and control functions by providing conference areas, video-teleconferencing, digital data transmission and reception, and public information and/or media outlet dissemination. A modern MCV serves this need.

Emergency Vehicles, Inc. (EVI), located at 705 13th Street, Lake Park, Florida 33403, was selected as the vendor for this project.

In Fiscal Year 2024/2025, the CWPB allocated $980,000 for the purchase

During the design, procurement, and purchase process, the cost of the project incurred increases from the initial cost quote/estimates. The balance of the project was initially going to be covered by using State Forfeiture Trust funds; however, guidelines for purchasing capital assets do not permit this type of transaction. The additional project funding of $150,416 will be allocated from reserves (Fund 327- Fleet Replacement Fund) to amend the fleet replacement fund to finalize and complete the purchase.

Ordinance/Resolution

RESOLUTION NO. 141-25: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, AMENDING A PREVIOUSLY APPROVED CLASS A SPECIAL USE PERMIT, TO CONSTRUCT A DOCK AT 7405 SOUTH FLAGLER DRIVE; DECLARING THE DEVELOPMENT TO BE CONSISTENT WITH THE COMPREHENSIVE PLAN OF THE CITY; GRANTING WAIVERS TO THE ZONING AND LAND DEVELOPMENT REGULATIONS; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

Staff Recommended Motion

Approve Resolution No. 141-25 for a Class A Special Use Permit to amend a previously approved Class A Special Use Permit, to allow for the construction of a 200-foot-long private dock with two (2) boat lifts at 7405 S. Flagler Drive.

City Commission - Jul 21 2025

This motion is based upon the application submitted; the staff report; factual testimony; the recommendation of the Planning Board; the findings that the request complies with the Comprehensive Plan; and the standards in Section 94-36(e)(3),(4) and (5), Sections 94-313(a)(3) and (4), and Section 94-273(a)(2), of the City’s Zoning and Land Development Regulations.

Background Information

(THE INFORMATION PROVIDED BELOW IS A GENERAL SUMMARY.

A FULL ANALYSIS IS INCLUDED IN THE STAFF REPORT.)

The subject property is located at 7405 South Flagler Drive and is comprised of property on both the east and west sides of South Flagler Drive. The western portion of the property consists of a single-family residential dwelling unit. The cumulative acreage of the eastern and western portions of the subject property is 0.4 acres.

Pursuant to Section 94-313(a) of the Zoning and Land Development Regulations (ZLDRs), a Class A Special Use Permit is required when waivers to the City’s standards for dock design are requested. Accessory docks are also permitted by right on properties possessing contiguous and non-contiguous riparian rights. The subject property possesses noncontiguous riparian rights to the Intracoastal Waterway. The dock will extend into the waterway within the middle one-third of the lot.

Section 94-313(a)(3)(i) of the City’s ZLDRs requires that all residential docks not exceed 100 feet in length. On January 8, 2024, the property owner obtained City Commission approval of Resolution No. 324-23 for a Class A Special Use Permit to construct a dock measuring 136 feet and 9 inches in length with no boat lift on the subject property.

The applicant requests to amend the previously approved Class A Special Use Permit with a Waiver, to allow the construction of a 200-footlong private dock with two (2) boat lifts. This amendment requires a waiver of 63 feet, three (3) inches, to ensure that the dock can accommodate vessels while maintaining the minimum water depth required for mooring, particularly during low tide.

Currently, the properties directly located to the north and south of the subject site along South Flagler Drive have non-contiguous riparian rights into the Intracoastal Waterway with docks exceeding the permitted 136 feet and 9 inches in length, further supporting the request to extend the previously approved dock on the subject property.

Although the proposed dock will be one of the longest overall docks in the area, the portions of the dock structure located in the water will fall within the length of other docks in the vicinity of the subject property. One of the

City Commission - Jul 21 2025

primary reasons for amending the previously approved dock extension is the unique geography of the site. As identified in Picture 1 and Picture 2 of the Staff Report, the sandy area along the shoreline of the subject property is notably wider and shallower compared to other areas of the beach, requiring the dock structure to extend further in order to reach the minimum water depths required by code. This adjustment is necessary to accommodate bathymetric conditions that are suitable for safe navigation and docking.

CONCLUSION

The provision of a waiver for the maximum length required by Section 94313(a)(3)(i)(i) would reasonably accommodate water vessels, while avoiding maintenance dredging, which could wash away upland shorelines. It is staff’s professional opinion that the additional dock length will not have an adverse effect on the surrounding properties and is necessary to provide the required protection. With the exception of the dock’s length, staff finds that the request satisfies the required provisions of the ZLDRs. Therefore, staff finds the request satisfies all the standards found within the ZLDRs and, as such, is recommending approval, subject to the conditions contained in Resolution No. 141-25.

After a Public Hearing on May 20, 2025, the Planning Board unanimously recommended approval (6-0) of the Class A Special Use Permit to the City Commission.
