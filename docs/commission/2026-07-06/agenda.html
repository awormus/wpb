The City of West Palm Beach is committed to civility and decorum by its officials, employees and members of the public who attend this meeting. The City Code, Secs. 2-31(8), 2-31(18) and 2-31(22), provides in pertinent part:
- Officials shall be recognized by the Chair and shall not interrupt a speaker.
- Public comment shall be addressed to the City Commission as a whole and not to any individual on the dais or in the audience.

Background Information

Pursuant to Section 401.113, Florida Statutes, Palm Beach County may distribute funds that it receives from the State Department of Health to emergency medical providers within Palm Beach County to improve and expand emergency medical services, as deemed appropriate by the Board of County Commissioners.

The grant funds will be provided to the City by Palm Beach County on a reimbursement basis once the medical equipment has been purchased.

The Fire Department does not currently use automated dispensing cabinets. As a result, all personnel who will operate the machines will require training.

Resolution No. 132-26 accepts the grant and authorizes the use of the grant funds to purchase the EMS equipment.

9.2. Public Hearing and Second Reading of Ordinance No. 5173-26: A Cityinitiated request for a text amendment to Chapter 94 Zoning and Land Development Regulations, Article VII - Special Districts, Section 94-219 Broadway mixed-use district to clarify provisions regarding the development of townhomes; and Section 94-215 - Currie mixed-use district to modify the height bonus incentive requirements for improvements to Currie Park.

Originating Department

Development Services

Ordinance/Resolution

ORDINANCE NO. 5173-26: AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, AMENDING CHAPTER 94 ZONING AND LAND DEVELOPMENT REGULATIONS, ARTICLE VII - SPECIAL DISTRICTS, SECTION 94-219 - BROADWAY MIXED-USE DISTRICT TO CLARIFY PROVISIONS REGARDING THE DEVELOPMENT OF TOWNHOMES; AND SECTION 94-215 - CURRIE MIXED-USE DISTRICT TO MODIFY THE HEIGHT BONUS INCENTIVE REQUIREMENTS FOR IMPROVEMENTS TO CURRIE PARK; PROVIDING A CONFLICTS CLAUSE AND A SEVERABILITY CLAUSE; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

Staff Recommended Motion

Approve Ordinance No. 5173-26. This motion is based on the staff report, factual testimony, the recommendation of the Planning Board, and the findings that the proposed amendment complies with the amendment standards in Section 94-32 of the City's Zoning and Land Development Regulations.

Background Information

The City-initiated amendments proposed are divided into two (2) different topics. The first one impacts the Broadway Mixed-Use District (BMUD), and the second one impacts the Currie Mixed-Use District (CMUD) as follows:

A. AMENDMENTS TO BROADWAY MIXED-USE DISTRICT

BMUD is a mixed-use redevelopment area comprised of approximately 43.9 acres, generally located north of 25th Street, south of the northern City limits and to the east and west of Broadway. The Corridor abuts other established residential areas within the City, such as the West Northwood, Northboro Park, and Old Northwood Historic Districts.

Last year, the City approved an amendment to the BMUD regulations that created a revised set of zoning regulations guided by the following principles:
- Promote the redevelopment of the corridor as a mixed-use corridor with emphasis on residential uses.

  • Protect the single-family residential fabric adjacent to the corridor.

  • Improve the quality of the public realm to create an attractive activity center to fulfill the daily needs of residents within walking distance.

The adopted regulations allow residential and commercial use and include a housing incentive to encourage the construction of residential uses with workforce housing units.

The intent of the adopted regulations is to allow multifamily residential uses, including townhouse developments.

After the adoption of the regulations, it has been identified that certain provisions may conflict with the construction of townhouse projects for sale.

Staff is proposing some minor adjustments to ensure townhouses can be built. The proposed changes include:

Adjustment of provisions regarding mid-block setbacks to clarify that midblock setbacks are not required for townhouses. 2. Clarify language to allow steps/stoops to be located within the primary streetscape improvement area, but outside the clear pedestrian path.

B. CURRIE MIXED-USE DISTRICT AMENDMENTS

CMUD is a compact, pedestrian-oriented mixed-use redevelopment area generally located between North Flagler Drive to the east and North Dixie Highway to the west, 27th Street to the north, and 14th Street to the south.

The area is divided into several sub-districts, and each sub-district includes specific development characteristics and maximum height allowed. In addition to the maximum base height for each subdistrict, the zoning regulations also include a series of incentives to allow increases from the base height in exchange for public benefits. Within the CMUD zoning regulations, Table VII-16 – Height Bonus Incentives specifies the allowable bonus height incentive permitted for each sub-district and lists the different public benefits that can be selected.

One of the public benefits offered to Developers is the improvements to Currie Park. For this incentive, the Developer will receive one foot of additional height above their base per $25,000 in credit for the construction of improvement or payments made to Currie Park. The exact language states: Section 94-215(K)(2)(i) Improvements to Currie Park.

The developer seeking to utilize this incentive will receive one foot of height per $25,000 in credit for the actual construction of improvements or payments made to Currie Park.

These improvements must be approved by the City/CRA prior to commencement of construction and must be consistent with the improvements planned by the City/CRA for Currie Park. If the proposed improvement is not part of the original concept approved by the City/CRA then approval must be sought before the credit will be provided to the developer.

Since the adoption of the CMUD regulations, the City has completed the redesign of the park, and the construction of the improvements is currently underway. Considering the construction of the park is already moving forward, the City would like to modify the language in the incentive to allow additional flexibility in the use of the funds. It is expected that once the park is completed, funding will be needed for the operations, management, and programming of the park. In addition, the proposed amendment clearly specifies the time when the contribution shall be paid by the Developer.

The staff report is included as supporting documentation to the agenda item.

The proposed amendments were presented to the Planning Board during a public hearing on January 21, 2026, and a recommendation of approval (5-0) was received.

The proposed amendments impact properties located within Commission District 1: Commissioner Cathleen Ward.

V. BACKGROUND AND ANALYSIS

The subject site, which is currently in unincorporated Palm Beach County, is developed with an existing 5,408 square foot church building constructed in 1964, and an 8,482 square foot church building constructed ten (10) years later in 1974, directly north of the original construction. Together, the church campus totals 13,890 square feet and accommodates 255 seats.

In 2002, the applicant sought development approvals from the County to expand the campus through additional worship and educational facilities. As such, the site was rezoned from the County’s Residential High Density (RH) to Residential Medium Density (RM) pursuant to County Resolution No. 2002-134. Concurrently, a Class A Conditional Use (CA) was approved for a church or place of worship through Resolution No. 2002-135.

The approved plan provided for the construction of a new 24,832-square-foot church building with seating for 558 people. The new church was never constructed; however, the site remains developed with only the existing 13,890-square-foot Church improvements, with most of the site area to the east remaining vacant and undeveloped with minimal vegetation.

In addition to the proposed Annexation, Future Land Use (FLU) Amendment and Rezoning which are the cases in front of the City Commission at this hearing, the applicant is also seeking approval of a Special Site Plan Review pursuant to Sec. 94-552 under the Affordable and Workforce Housing Overlay (AWHO) program to approve 318 multifamily residential units, including twenty-two (22) Workforce Housing (WFH) units and a waiver request for a reduction of the parking provided. The Special Site Plan Review is to be approved by Staff, and the waiver request is scheduled to go to the City Commission concurrent with the adoption hearing for the Annexation, FLU Amendment and Rezoning, and is not addressed in this staff report. The name of the proposed development is Alton West Palm Beach.

The various aspects of the application are discussed in detail below.

Ordinance No. 5174-26 - Annexation The applicant is requesting a voluntary annexation of the subject parcel, which totals 8.54 acres. The parcel is located at the Northeast corner of Haverhill Road and 45th Street. The properties to the south of the subject site, on the south side of 45th Street, are within the limits of the City of West Palm Beach. The properties to the north, east, and west are, and will remain, within unincorporated Palm Beach County.

The proposed annexation meets the criteria for voluntary annexation, pursuant to Chapter 171, Sections 171.043 and 171.044, Florida Statutes. In addition, on October 23, 2025, the Palm Beach County Department of Planning, Zoning & Building issued a letter indicating that, after reviewing the proposed annexation, it had not identified any inconsistencies with Chapter 171, Florida Statutes.

Ordinance No. 5175-26 - Future Land Use Map Designation Assignment Upon annexation into the City, the property must be assigned a City FLU designation.

The County’s current FLU designation of Medium Residential-5 (MR-5) allows residential uses, including multifamily. Since the applicant is seeking to build 318 multifamily residential units on the site, the applicant is requesting a Multifamily (MF) FLU designation, which appears appropriate for this type of proposal. Attachment V includes maps showing the current and proposed FLU designation for the subject site and the current FLU designations for adjacent parcels.

Pursuant to Chapter 163 of the Florida Statutes, each level of service (LOS) element (i.e. drainage, potable water, wastewater, solid waste, school, and traffic) for any proposed FLU Map amendment shall be reviewed and analyzed by the City to determine if the new FLU designation, utilizing the maximum development potential, would cause any increase in impact, and if so, whether or not each public facility has the reserved capacity to accommodate the additional demand. The applicant prepared an LOS table that compares the existing and proposed FLU designations and their respective maximum development potential for the subject property. The table indicates an increase in water, wastewater, solid waste, and traffic impacts based on the additional density from the proposed City FLU designation as compared to the existing Palm Beach County FLU designation. The table shows that these LOS increases do not exceed the service provider capacities. Planning staff has confirmed the analysis.

The proposed assignment of the MF FLU designation meets two (2) of the Future Land Use Amendment Standards found in Future Land Use Element Policy 1.1.5 of the City’s Comprehensive Plan: Changed Assumptions and New Issues.

Ordinance No. 5176-26 - Zoning Map Designation Assignment Upon annexation into the City and the assignment of a City MF FLU designation, the property must be assigned a City zoning designation.

Multifamily high-density (MF32) is an appropriate zoning designation within an MF FLU designation.

The proposed assignment of the MF32 zoning designation complies with all eight (8) Rezoning Standards found in Section 94-32 of the City’s Zoning and Land Development Regulations.

Background Information

The City's Flagler Drive corridor is currently undergoing rapid transformation with the redevelopment of older multifamily/condo buildings, which is reshaping the skyline and neighborhood character. This transformation prompted the City Commission to adopt Ordinance No. 5139-25, which established new regulations for the Multifamily High Density MF(32) Residential zoning district along the North Flagler Drive corridor, to manage the scale of redevelopment in the area. The new regulations prohibit any new planned development.

Staff is now proposing to evaluate the existing zoning conditions for the South Flagler corridor, for the MF(32) zoned properties located between Monroe Drive and Southern Boulevard in proximity to low-density singlefamily homes and duplexes, south of Washington Road.

To complete this evaluation, the City is in the process of hiring Zyscovich to do a zoning analysis, redevelopment testing, preparation of development framework recommendations, economic analysis, and drafting of zoning regulations. The Zoning in Progress will expire on January 20, 2027, unless it is extended by the City Commission.
