4. CIVILITY AND DECORUM

The City of West Palm Beach is committed to civility and decorum by its officials, employees and members of the public who attend this meeting. The City Code, Secs. 2-31(8), 2-31(18) and 2-31(22), provides in pertinent part:
- Officials shall be recognized by the Chair and shall not interrupt a speaker.
- Public comment shall be addressed to the City Commission as a whole and not to any individual on the dais or in the audience.
- Displays of anger, rudeness, ridicule, impatience, lack of respect and personal attacks are strictly prohibited.
- Unauthorized remarks from the audience, stamping of feet, whistles, yells and similar demonstrations shall not be permitted.
- Offenders may be removed from the meeting.
- Any person desiring to address the Commission shall file a written request with the city clerk prior to consideration of the matter by the Commission or prior to the public comment portion of a meeting. The person wishing to speak shall complete a comment card for each agenda item the person wishes to address, which shall include the person's full name, address, and the numbered agenda item. The person will not be recognized if the comment card is not completed.

8.1. Resolution No. 143-25(F) accepting and appropriating funds in the amount of $5,500 from Prime Time of Palm Beach County funded by the Children Services Council in recognition of the after-school programs at Gaines Park, Vedado Park, Pleasant City, and South Olive Park completing the Quality Improvement System (QIS) core quality practices.

Originating Department

Parks and Recreation

Ordinance/Resolution

RESOLUTION NO. 143-25(F): A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, AUTHORIZING THE APPROPRIATION OR TRANSFER OF CITY FUNDS IN FISCAL YEAR 2024/2025 IN ACCORDANCE WITH SECTION 4.03 OF THE CITY CHARTER OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, FOR THE PURPOSE OF AMENDING THE DONATION FUND TO PROVIDE FOR THE RECEIPT AND APPROPRIATION OF THE GRANT AWARD FROM PRIME TIME OF PALM BEACH COUNTY TO PROVIDE AFTER SCHOOL PROGRAM SUPPLIES, PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT, FIELD TRIPS, SPECIAL YOUTH/FAMILY EVENTS, OTHER PROGRAM EXPENDITURES; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

Background Information

The Quality Improvement System (QIS) incentive program is a Prime Time of Palm Beach County strategy designed to further increase afterschool program quality for their participants' activities/programs. The QIS is a performance-based recognition program that awards financial incentives to out-of-school time programs that go above and beyond in their QIS work. The award amount is based upon a tiered process through which a program demonstrates the implementation of advanced youth development and program management practices. These practices are in concert and direct relationship with a program's voluntary participation and work in Prime Time's QIS incentive program. Funding is provided by the Children's Services Council of Palm Beach County and is based upon the availability of funds and performance.

This past year, four (4) of the City's after-school programs were eligible to receive a QIS Core Practices Development Grant through Prime Time's QIS incentive program. They have each completed the QIS core quality practices that aim to increase and maintain positive youth development, which leads young people to develop skills necessary to be successful socially, emotionally, and academically. Prime Time requires that the grant be spent on professional development, program supplies, field trips, special youth and/or family events, and other program expenditures.

City Commission - Jul 07 2025

Gaines Park Community Center, South Olive Community Center, Pleasant City Community Center, and Vedado Park Community Center have each been recognized for going above and beyond in their QIS work. Pleasant City K-5th grade was awarded $1,500; Gaines Park 6th8th grade was awarded $1,500; Vedado Park 6th-8th grade was awarded $1,250; and South Olive K-5th grade was awarded $1,250.

Resolution No. 143-25(F) accepts $5,500 from Prime Time of Palm Beach County, funded by the Children Services Council, in recognition of the after-school programs completing the Quality Improvement System (QIS) core quality practices.

Originating Department

Fire

Ordinance/Resolution

RESOLUTION NO. 163-25(F): A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, AUTHORIZING THE APPROPRIATION OR TRANSFER OF CITY FUNDS IN THE AMOUNT OF $36,211 FROM THE 2024/2025 FISCAL YEAR IN ACCORDANCE WITH SECTION 4.03 OF THE CITY CHARTER OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, FOR THE PURPOSE OF AMENDING THE CAPITAL ACQUISITION FUND BUDGET TO RE-APPROPRIATE PROJECT FUNDING FROM FIRE STATION NO. 4 TO THE FUEL TANK REPLACEMENT PROJECT; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

Background Information

The diesel Aboveground Storage Tank (AST) serves as a critical fuel source during power outages, such as those caused by hurricanes. It supplies fuel for the fire station’s emergency generator and for refueling fire apparatus.

City Commission - Jul 07 2025

Fueling of the fire apparatus at the fire station offers several benefits. It ensures that each unit remains within its primary response zone while refueling, thereby enhancing unit availability and improving response times within its designated coverage area. The new tank also offers improved functionality, featuring double the capacity of the previous tank, compliance with current safety standards, and a cylindrical design that reduces maintenance costs.

Funds remaining from the design of Fire Station No. 4, located at 1718 Parker Avenue, in Account 304.032710.522.620, will be used to support this project.

A total of $36,211 will be transferred to Account 304.032710.522.5000640, which currently has a balance of $148,250.

Gulfstream Petroleum Services Inc. was selected through the Invitation to Bid process (ITB 24.25.122) and was awarded the contract on May 29, 2025. The total contract amount is $178,400.

Fiscal Note

Approval of this item re-appropriates project funding from Fire Station No. 4 to fund the remaining Fuel Tank Replacement project.

Resolution No. 167-25 approves, as Face of the City, the Revolutionary War Patriots Memorial Marker to honor the birth of the United States of America and to be installed at Monceaux Park, 2994 South Flagler Drive by the Daughters of the Revolution (DAR), Henry Morrison Flagler Chapter. Privately funded.

Originating Department

Mayor's Office

Ordinance/Resolution

RESOLUTION NO. 167-25: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA APPROVING THE REVOLUTIONARY WAR PATRIOTS MEMORIAL MARKER TO BE INSTALLED AT 2994 SOUTH FLAGLER DRIVE AT MONCEAUX PARK AND PRIVATELY FUNDED IN COMPLIANCE WITH THE PUBLIC ART ORDINANCE; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND OTHER PURPOSES.

Staff Recommended Motion

Approve Resolution No. 167-25.

This motion is based on the findings that the proposed memorial marker meets the criteria set forth in Section 78-125 of the City Code as reviewed by the ArtLife WPB Committee.

Background Information

On June 4, 2025, at the regular scheduled meeting, the ArtLife Committee reviewed the Monument and Memorial Applications submitted by the Henry Morrison Flagler Chapter of the Daughters of the Revolution (DAR) under Section 78-10 - Article 1 General.

The National Society Daughters of the American Revolution (NSDAR) was founded in 1890 to promote Historic Preservation, education, and patriotism across the United States. The NSDAR’s volunteer work is

City Commission - Jul 07 2025

accomplished through the grassroots efforts of its chapters at the local level. The Henry Morrison Flagler Chapter is comprised of eighty (80) members living in West Palm Beach and eastern Palm Beach County.

In honor of our nation’s 250th anniversary, the DAR local chapter is requesting to place a historic marker in the City of West Palm Beach to commemorate our Nation’s Revolutionary War Patriots. This effort is part of a larger national activity to honor those who paved the way to create the United States of America. The local chapter states, “ West Palm Beach is a city that honors its’ past and present Patriots for all National holidays, and this marker would become a permanent reminder to the community of West Palm of the Patriots who fought for the freedoms we enjoy today.”

The bronze marker is a stand-alone 30” x 30” to be installed at Monceaux Park, 2994 S. Flagler Drive. The marker, delivery, installation, and maintenance of the memorial marker will be privately funded.

Resolution No. 167-25 approves, as Face of the City, the “Revolutionary War Patriots” memorial marker to be installed at Monceaux Park, 2994 S.

Flagler Drive.

10. PUBLIC HEARING

10.1. Public Hearing and First Reading of Ordinance No. 5136-25 amending the Code of Ordinances at Chapter 86 to aid in parking enforcement of oversized vehicles.

Originating Department

Mayor's Office

Ordinance/Resolution

ORDINANCE NO. 5136-25: AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, AMENDING THE CODE OF ORDINANCES AT CHAPTER 86 (TRAFFIC AND PARKING), ARTICLE I (IN GENERAL), SECTION 86-1 (DEFINITIONS) TO ADD NEW DEFINITIONS; AMENDING ARTICLE IV (STOPPING, STANDING, AND PARKING), SECTION 86-229 TO STREAMLINE THE ENFORCEMENT OF PARKING VIOLATIONS INVOLVING CERTAIN VEHICLES WITHIN RESIDENTIAL DISTRICTS AND AMENDING THE PENALTY PROVISION; PROVIDING A CONFLICTS CLAUSE, A SEVERABILITY CLASE, A CODIFICATION CLAUSE AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

City Commission - Jul 07 2025

Staff Recommended Motion

Approve Ordinance No. 5136-25 on First Reading and schedule Second Reading for July 21, 2025.

Background Information

Pursuant to Article VIII, Section (2)(b) of the Florida Constitution and Chapter 166, Florida Statutes, the City of West Palm Beach has broad home rule powers to adopt ordinances to regulate parking on City public rights-of-way, to establish and operate a parking system and a transit system, and to require permits and establish fees related to such systems and regulations.

The development of comprehensive transportation mobility strategies, plans, and policies, including parking and related mobility strategies, will help the City of West Palm Beach grow while becoming a safer, healthier, resilient, connected, and welcoming community.

Well-designed parking systems and policies ensure the continued health and vibrancy of residential districts.

The regulation of parking on City public rights-of-way is a valid municipal purpose and serves the public welfare.

Chapter 86 of the City Code of Ordinances regulates, among other things, the types of vehicles that may be parked in residential districts.

The City desires to amend Chapter 86 as set forth to prohibit and restrict the parking of certain types of vehicles in residential areas.

Fiscal Note

No fiscal impact.
