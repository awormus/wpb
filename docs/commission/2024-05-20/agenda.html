2. CIVILITY AND DECORUM

The City of West Palm Beach is committed to civility and decorum by its officials, employees and members of the public who attend this meeting. The City Code, Secs. 2-31(8), 2-31(18) and 2-31(22), provides in pertinent part:
- Officials shall be recognized by the Chair and shall not interrupt a speaker.
- Public comment shall be addressed to the City Commission as a whole and not to any individual on the dais or in the audience.
- Displays of anger, rudeness, ridicule, impatience, lack of respect and personal attacks are strictly prohibited.
- Unauthorized remarks from the audience, stamping of feet, whistles, yells and similar demonstrations shall not be permitted.
- Offenders may be removed from the meeting.
- Any person desiring to address the Commission shall file a written request with the city clerk prior to consideration of the matter by the Commission or prior to the public comment portion of a meeting. The person wishing to speak shall complete a comment card for each agenda item the person wishes to address, which shall include the person's full name, address, and the numbered agenda item. The person will not be recognized if the comment card is not completed.

Originating Department

Background Information

In December 1924, representatives from ten (10) state municipal leagues convened at the University of Kansas in Lawrence, Kansas, laying the foundation for what we now recognize as the National League of Cities (NLC). For close to a century, the NLC has remained steadfast in its mission to champion local governance, advocating tirelessly for the interests of cities, towns, and villages throughout the United States.

Marking this significant centennial milestone, the NLC launched the "NLC Centennial Roadshow: 100 Years, 100 Cities," a year-long initiative aimed at commemorating the organization's legacy.

Throughout this journey, the NLC will visit 100 cities, both in-person and virtually, spotlighting the diverse narratives of municipalities across the nation and honoring the invaluable contributions of local governments and their residents over the past century. The roadshow commenced in March 2024 in Lawrence, Kansas, the birthplace of the NLC, and will culminate in November during the organization's annual City Summit in Tampa, Florida.

5.2. American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) Update Presentation

Originating Department

Mayor's Office

Background Information

The American Rescue Plan (ARPA) is legislation that was passed by the United States Congress and signed into law by President Joe Biden in March 2021.

Special City Commission - May 20 2024

ARPA included provisions for stimulus payments to individuals and families, extended unemployment benefits, aid to small business, and assistance for state and local governments.

The intended goal of ARPA was to mitigate the economic impact of the pandemic and help facilitate recovery efforts.
