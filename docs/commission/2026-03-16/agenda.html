Ordinance/Resolution

Background Information

Since 2007, the City of West Palm Beach has provided increased law enforcement presence in the County's waterways in partnership with the Board of County Commissioners of Palm Beach County, as approved in a Manatee Protection Plan and as one of the thirteen (13) "key" manatee counties identified by the Florida Governor and Cabinet in October 1989.

Studies by the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission have demonstrated that increased law enforcement presence is the most effective means of gaining compliance with boater speed zones and manatee protection speed zones, and of reducing the risks to manatees and members of the public.

In accordance with the Interlocal Agreement for Law Enforcement Services for manatee protection between Palm Beach County and the City of West Palm Beach approved by City Commission November 27, 2023 (WPB Contract No. 21861.001), the West Palm Beach Police Department will enforce State statutes, administrative rules, and local and County ordinances related to vessel control, marine resource protection, and manatee protection and assist in educating boaters in the enforcement area about manatee and boater safety issues.

7.4. Resolution No. 67-26(F) approving the transfer of $206,197 of City funds in Fiscal Year 2025/2026 for the purpose of amending the Grants Capital Project fund budget to provide additional appropriations for the Currie Park Redevelopment project.

Originating Department

Housing and Community Development

Ordinance/Resolution

RESOLUTION NO. 67-26(F): A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, AUTHORIZING THE APPROPRIATION OR TRANSFER OF CITY FUNDS IN FISCAL YEAR 2025/2026 IN ACCORDANCE WITH SECTION 4.03 OF THE CITY CHARTER OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, FOR THE PURPOSE OF AMENDING THE COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT FUND AND GRANT CAPITAL PROJECT FUND BUDGETS TO PROVIDE FOR APPROPRIATIONS FOR THE CURRIE PARK REDEVELOPMENT PROJECT; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

Background Information

The City of West Palm Beach has prioritized the revitalization of Currie Park as part of its broader strategy to enhance public spaces, promote community engagement, and stimulate economic development along the City’s waterfront. Located in the Currie Corridor of the Community Redevelopment Agency (CRA), the 13-acre waterfront park is currently approved and funded for $35.3 million in renovations. Renovations include: flood and stormwater mitigation work, the inclusion of a tidal amphitheater, a waterfront lagoon, a beach area, and other shoreline restoration and access infrastructure. Additional enhancements will include a playground, pedestrian/bicyclist trails, fitness lawn, and other community recreation and resilience facilities and features.

To support this transformative initiative, by Resolution No. 135-25(F), approved by City Commission on June 9, 2025, the Housing and Community Development Department (HCD) allocated $1,287,996 in Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) funding to the Currie Park Redevelopment Project. HCD desires to allocate additional funding of $206,197 in CDBG funding to the project. These funds were originally appropriated in the City’s Community Development Fund. Governmental Accounting Standard Board (GASB) Statement Number 54 restricts the use of Special Revenue Funds to account for revenue and expenditures for purposes other than capital projects. It is acceptable for Special Revenue Fund 110, the Community Development Fund, to provide funds for eligible capital improvement projects. However, capital purchases cannot be expensed in Fund 110, per government accounting requirements. Therefore, it is required that funds be transferred out from Fund 110 to the Grant Capital Project Fund 399, so the capital payments can be expensed and reported properly in financial statements.

City Commission - Mar 16 2026

Resolution No. 162-23, Resolution No. 73-25, and Resolution No. 9525(F) amended the Annual One-Year Action Plans for Program Years 2020 through 2025 for the CDBG to reallocate funding to eligible public facilities/improvement projects. A portion of these funds will be used for the Currie Park Redevelopment project.

On April 7, 2025, HCD entered into an Inter-Department Subrecipient Agreement with the Engineering Department for the use of CDBG funds for the Currie Park Redevelopment project. The Inter-Department Subrecipient Agreement is being amended to increase the allocation by $206,197. The project is eligible under the CDBG program regulations, as per 24 CFR 570.210(c), and meets the National Objective of Area Benefit Activities, as defined by 24 CFR 570.208(a)(1).

Resolution No. 67-26(F) approves the transfer of the grant funds.

Background Information

The State of Florida, Department of Financial Services, approved the Florida Firefighter Technical Equipment Grant application and awarded the City of West Palm Beach Fire Department a $254,766 grant for the purchase of dive rescue-related equipment. Some examples of dive equipment to be purchased include: Viking Haztech Drysuits, OTS MK-III Diver Communication Systems, Gemini Switch Blocks, Surface Support Equipment, and ZOLL Monitors.

The purchase of the equipment will keep the firefighters safe from contaminated water, enable communication in the most challenging underwater environments, and ensure divers' safety and uninterrupted air supply during emergency situations. It will also provide rapid, accurate detection of hazardous substances, including toxic chemicals, explosives, and narcotics. The Zoll EKG monitors will provide the latest technology to assist in monitoring a patient’s cardiac rhythm and deliver defibrillation for life-threatening arrhythmias. Zoll monitors are a vital component of patient assessment and will be used on all patients during cardiac arrest, including patients seen by paramedics.

Additionally, the purchase of dive equipment will improve emergency response time by several minutes and ensure more equipped divers are spread throughout the City. It will allow divers to deploy life-saving measures more rapidly and will have a significant impact on the response to high-profile events and locations such as Mar-a-Lago, the convention center, and other venues.

As a condition of the grant, the City of West Palm Beach is required to contribute $382,150 in addition to accepting the grant award, for a total budget of $636,916.

Resolution No. 58-26 accepts the State Grant Award, and Resolution No. 66-26(F) recognizes the grant funding and opioid settlement, to be used for the purchase of dive-related and other fire technical equipment.

Staff Recommended Motion

Approve Ordinance No. 5166-26 on Second Reading.

NOTE: Section 22-31(27) of the Code of Ordinances requires that the sale shall be approved by ordinance and such ordinance shall be approved by a super majority (4/5) vote of the City Commission at either First Reading or Second Reading. This requirement was met at First Reading.

Background Information

The City of West Palm Beach is the owner of certain real property located at 202, 204, and 206 North Sapodilla, consisting of approximately 0.79 acres, more or less (the “Property”), currently vacant and zoned Quadrille Business District-3.

By Resolution No. 272-25, the City Commission declared the property to be surplus, finding that it was not needed for City purposes, and authorized negotiations with Publix Super Markets, Inc. (“Publix”), for its sale for use as a supermarket.

Publix desires to relocate its current supermarket within the West Palm Beach downtown to the Property to allow development of a more expansive store. Publix is under contract to purchase the adjacent properties, which comprise the remainder of the block.

A Phase I environmental report indicated the existence of several contaminants on the Property resulting from the Property’s prior uses, which included a railroad spur, a manufactured gas plant, and a coal storage area.

City staff and Publix have negotiated the sale of the Property subject to the following key terms and conditions: Purchase Price: The purchase price of Three Million Five Hundred Thousand Six Hundred Eighty-Four Dollars ($3,526,684) represents 85% of the appraised values, adjusted for environmental remediation, in compliance with Section 2-31(27)(c) of the Code of Ordinances.

Supermarket Use Restriction: Publix intends to construct and operate a full-service supermarket of a minimum of 40,000 square feet, offering a wide variety of groceries, including fresh produce, meats, dairy, beer and wine, pantry staples, plus household goods and non-food items, including a pharmacy, with associated parking.

A restrictive covenant shall require the Property to be used for a supermarket for a term of fifteen (15) years. Other uses will be allowed on the Property so long as the supermarket remains in operation.

Local Workforce: Publix shall use all commercially reasonable efforts to inform and hire at least fifteen percent (15%) of the on-site construction labor workforce from City residents.

Small Business Participation: Publix shall use all commercially reasonable efforts to engage no less than eighteen percent (18%) of its contractors and subcontractors from small businesses certified by the City.

Mineral Rights: Section 270.11, Florida Statutes, provides that unless the City chooses not to reserve its interest, deeds for the sale of land shall reserve to the City an undivided three-fourths interest in, and title in and to an undivided three-fourths interest in, all the phosphate, minerals and metals that are or may be in, on or under the said land and an undivided one-half interest in all the petroleum that is or may be in, on or under said land with the privilege to mine and develop the same. The City has no indication that any such minerals exist on the property and has no intent to enter the Property to mine for minerals.

The City will release its statutory interest with the deed.

Ordinance No. 5166-26 approves the sale of the Property to Publix and authorizes the Mayor to execute the Purchase and Sale Agreement, which contains the key terms, and all closing documents.

The purchase price revenue will be recorded in the City’s General Fund (GF) real estate sales account.
