9.2. Resolution No. 148-26 approves an interlocal agreement between the City of West Palm Beach and Saint Lucie County to share public safety radio interoperability.

Originating Department

Police

Ordinance/Resolution

RESOLUTION NO. 148-26: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, APPROVING AN INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH AND ST. LUCIE COUNTY FOR SHARED USE OF PUBLIC SAFETY RADIO COMMUNICATIONS INFRASTRUCTURE; PROVIDING FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND OTHER PURPOSES.

Background Information

The West Palm Beach Police Department (WPBPD) has several interlocal agreements with local counties and municipalities granting access to their respective public safety radio communication networks. The interlocal agreements grant permission to program WPBPD public safety radio devices with the outside agencies' public safety radio network.

Multi-county and regional radio interoperability ensures seamless, realtime communication across different public safety agencies and jurisdictions. It directly eliminates communication bottlenecks during largescale crises, mutual aid deployments, and task force operations, ultimately boosting response coordination and saving lives.
The most critical benefits include:
- Improved Crisis Response: During large-scale emergencies that span multiple counties (such as hurricanes, wildfires, or widespread severe weather), regional interoperability establishes a shared operating picture, allowing police, fire, and EMS to coordinate efforts and avoid delays.

  • Optimal Resource Allocation: When a local emergency outpaces an individual county's resources, interoperability makes it much easier to request and direct mutual aid, as dispatchers can track and route resources from neighboring jurisdictions.

  • Enhanced Situational Awareness: First responders can exchange critical information across agencies without relying on manual relays through dispatch. This means real-time sharing of incident details, suspect descriptions, and hazard warnings. Mutual Backup Redundancy: If one local master dispatch site or communications tower goes offline during a disaster, a broader regional network ensures that public safety communications can be rerouted through neighboring infrastructure without losing contact.

  • Streamlined Data Exchange: Many modern regional radio systems also bridge data, such as GPS locations and computer-aided dispatch (CAD) information, further aligning multi-agency responses.

The interlocal agreement has no fiscal impact and is not district-specific.

Resolution No. 148-26 approves the interlocal agreement with Saint Lucie County.

Fiscal Note

No fiscal impact.

Resolution No. 150-26 for the execution of a Utility Work by Highway Contractor Agreement (UWHCA) with the State of Florida Department of Transportation (FDOT) for incidental City Utility Work in Conjunction with State Road No. 5/US-1 Broadway Avenue Improvements Project (from 25th Street to 45th Street).

Originating Department

Public Utilities

Ordinance/Resolution

RESOLUTION NO. 150-26: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, APPROVING A UTILITY WORK BY HIGHWAY CONTRACTOR AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH AND FLORIDA DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION (FDOT) RELATING TO FDOT’S WORK ON STATE ROAD NO. 5/US-1 BROADWAY BETWEEN 25TH STREET AND 45TH STREET; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

Background Information

The Florida Department of Transportation (FDOT) will be undertaking the State Road No. 5/US-1 Broadway Avenue Improvements project from 25th Street to 45th Street (“FDOT Project"). The FDOT Project involves milling and resurfacing, sidewalk and driveway replacement, drainage improvements, lighting, and signalization improvements. Within the 1-mile length of the FDOT Project corridor, incidental improvements to the City's existing utilities will be required (Utility Work). The Utility Work involves the adjustment of sanitary sewer manhole lids, meter boxes, and water valve boxes to finished grade. The construction phase for the project is scheduled for 2029. According to FDOT, the timeline may be advanced to 2027 if funding becomes available.

FDOT and the City have determined that it is in the best interest of the public and to the economic advantage of both parties to enter into a Utility Work by Highway Contractor Agreement (UWHCA) to accomplish the City's utility improvements ("Agreement"). Accordingly, the City’s related utility work will be competitively bid as part of the FDOT project and constructed by the FDOT Contractor.

Under the terms of the Agreement, the City shall reimburse FDOT a total estimated amount of $127,456 for the cost of the Utility Work. This amount includes the cost of utility items plus ten percent (10%) construction contingency and two percent (2%) for the CEI (Construction Engineering & Inspection).

Resolution No. 150-26 approves the UWHCA with FDOT for the City’s incidental utility adjustments within State Road No. 5/US-1 Broadway Avenue Improvements project from 25th Street to 45th Street.

This project is located in Commission District 1: Commissioner Cathleen Ward.

Fiscal Note

The remaining available budget in the Water and Sewer Revenue Fund, Water Distribution Repair & Maintenance Services, after approval, is approximately $224,000.

The remaining available budget in the Water and Sewer Revenue Fund, Wastewater Collection/Sanitary Sewer Repair & Maintenance Services, after approval, is approximately $797,000.

10.1. Resolution No. 161-26 approving a Term Sheet with Pine Crest Preparatory School for the sale of approximately 57 acres of City property for the development of a school.

Originating Department

City Attorney's Office

Ordinance/Resolution

RESOLUTION NO. 161-26: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA APPROVING A TERM SHEET WITH PINE CREST PREPATORY SCHOOL, INC., FOR THE SALE OF APPROXIMATELY 57 ACRES LOCATED SOUTH OF ROEBUCK ROAD AND ADJACENT OT THE FLORIDA TURNPIKE FOR THE DEVELOPMENT OF A SCHOOL; WAIVING THE TIMING REQUIREMENT FOR THE APPRAISALS; AUTHORIZING ACCESS TO THE PROPERTY TO PERFORM DUE DILIGENCE INSPECTIONS; AUTHORIZING OWNER’S CONSENT FOR SUBMITTAL OF APPLICATIONS FOR LAND DEVELOPMENT APPROVALS; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

Staff Recommended Motion

Approve Resolution No. 161-26.

Background Information

Pine Crest Preparatory School, Inc. (“Pine Crest”), desire to develop, construct and contract for the operation of a school for students in kindergarten through grade 12, as such grades may be phased in, within the City.

Pine Crest desires to purchase from the City approximately 57 acres of the southern portion of City-owned property South of Roebuck Road and East of the Florida Turnpike (the “Property”). The adjacent northern City property contains a well field utilized by the City’s Public Utilities Department for water supply. By Resolution No. 54-25, the City Commission determined that the Property is not needed for City purposes and declared the Property to be surplus.

The City and Pine Crest have negotiated a Term Sheet establishing the material terms for the sale of the Property.

Section 2-31(27)(c) of the Code of Ordinances requires that two (2) appraisals be obtained for the Property, and that the price shall not be less than 85 percent (85%) of the average of the two (2) appraisals. The Code further requires that the appraisals not be earlier than six (6) months before the first reading of the ordinance approving the conveyance agreement, yet allows waiver of this provision by unanimous vote of the membership of the City Commission.

Two (2) appraisals were obtained in 2025 with an average appraised value of $6,625,000. By Resolution No. 54-25, the City Commission directed that more recent appraisals be obtained, but the City Commission would not consider a purchase price less than $6,625,000. Two (2) more recent appraisals were obtained, which considered various factors that reduced the appraised values.

The purchase price under the Pine Crest Term Sheet is based on the $6,625,000 value.

To promote access to quality education, Pine Crest provides financial aid to qualified students based on demonstrated financial need. Families with household incomes below the median for West Palm Beach would be expected to qualify for significant tuition assistance, up to 99% of tuition.

Pine Crest financial aid is need-based and customized to an individual family's financial profile. Families receiving tuition assistance at a level of 51% or greater will be eligible for inclusion aid. Inclusion aid, designed to enhance the overall experience and access to extracurriculars, covers expenses such as laptop computers, extended-day programs, field trips, enrichment, academic assistance, and transportation, when applicable. In addition, qualified West Palm Beach residents will also receive:
- Waived application fee;

  • Early preferred admission deadline and expedited admission decision notification;

  • Priority review and processing for financial aid; and

  • Access to targeted financial assistance available to qualified West Palm Beach residents.

The Term Sheet requires Pine Crest to provide the following additional community benefits: Contractors & Subcontractors: The School shall engage contractors and subcontractors from small businesses certified by the City at a value of not less than eighteen percent (18%) of the total construction costs.

Water Pressure: The School shall contribute an amount not to exceed $1,500,000 toward the costs of a pump station/water facility to enhance the water pressure to the intended school and surrounding community.

Pine Crest also seeks authorization from the City to perform due-diligence investigations while the Purchase and Sale Agreement is being drafted.

The Purchase and Sale Agreement will be brought back to the City Commission for approval.

The City has an interest in promoting access to quality education provided by both public and private schools. The City Commission may designate that the proceeds from this sale of City land may be used for the purposes of enhancing educational opportunities at Roosevelt Elementary School.

Resolution No. 161-26 approves the Term Sheet with Pine Crest Preparatory School, Inc.

Ordinance/Resolution

RESOLUTION NO. 172-26: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, APPROVING THE RECONFIGURATION OF DREHER TRAIL NORTH; GRANTING A LICENSE TO PERFORM THE ROADWAY RECONFIGURATION TO THE COX SCIENCE CENTER AND AQUARIUM, INC.; APPROVING AN AGREEMENT REGARDING DREHER TRAIL NORTH ROAD RECONFIGURATION; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

Staff Recommended Motion

Approve Resolution No. 172-26.

Background Information

The Cox Science Center and Aquarium, Inc. (“Science Center”), is undergoing an expansion of its facilities anticipated to significantly increase attendance and the school programs provided.

The Science Center and City agree that entry to the expanded Science Center for school buses and the general public will be improved by reconfiguring Dreher Trail North, which runs through Dreher Park. Such reconfiguration will include the elimination of traffic circles and the straightening of road curves along the west side of Dreher Park.

The reconfiguration of Dreher Trail North will also allow for additional parking for the Science Center while also providing more usable green space within Dreher Park.

On June 9, 2026, the Science Center's CEO, Kurt Allen, along with the design team, presented to the Parks and Recreation Advisory Committee (PRAC) the Dreher Trail North reconfiguration and improvements. PRAC voted unanimously to recommend approval.

The Science Center will receive a donation for the engineering, design, permitting, construction, inspections, and other related costs of the reconfiguration of Dreher Trail North, with such work being performed as part of the Science Center expansion.

Section 66-13 of the City of West Palm Beach Code of Ordinances requires City Commission approval of design features for above-ground changes involving streets, parks, buildings, or landscaping, referred to as the “Face of the City”.

The Science Center and City desire to enter into an agreement to provide for the reconfiguration of Dreher Trail North by the Science Center.

Resolution No. 172-26 approves the reconfiguration of Dreher Trail North and authorizes execution of the Agreement Regarding Dreher Trail North Road Reconfiguration with Cox Science Center and Aquarium, Inc.

V. BACKGROUND AND ANALYSIS

The subject site, which is currently in unincorporated Palm Beach County, is developed with an existing 5,408 square foot church building constructed in 1964, and an 8,482 square foot church building constructed ten (10) years later in 1974, directly north of the original construction. Together, the church campus totals 13,890 square feet and accommodates 255 seats.

In 2002, the applicant sought development approvals from the County to expand the campus through additional worship and educational facilities. As such, the site was rezoned from the County’s Residential High Density (RH) to Residential Medium Density (RM) pursuant to County Resolution No. 2002-134. Concurrently, a Class A Conditional Use (CA) was approved for a church or place of worship through Resolution No. 2002-135.

The approved plan provided for the construction of a new 24,832-square-foot church building with seating for 558 people. The new church was never constructed; however, the site remains developed with only the existing 13,890-square-foot Church improvements, with most of the site area to the east remaining vacant and undeveloped with minimal vegetation.

In addition to the proposed Annexation, FLU Amendment and Rezoning, which are the cases in front of the City Commission at this hearing, the applicant is also seeking approval of a Special Site Plan Review pursuant to Sec. 94-552 under the Affordable and Workforce Housing Overlay (AWHO) program to approve 318 multifamily residential units, including twenty-two (22) Workforce Housing (WFH) units and a waiver request for a reduction of the parking provided. The Special Site Plan Review is to be approved by Staff, and the waiver request is scheduled to go to the City Commission concurrent with the adoption hearing for the Annexation, FLU Amendment, and Rezoning, and is not addressed in this staff report. The name of the proposed development is Alton West Palm Beach.

The various aspects of the application are discussed in detail below.

Ordinance No. 5174-26 - Annexation The applicant is requesting a voluntary annexation of the subject parcel, which totals 8.54 acres. The parcel is located at the Northeast corner of Haverhill Road and 45th Street. The properties to the south of the subject site, on the south side of 45th Street, are within the limits of the City of West Palm Beach. The properties to the north, east, and west are, and will remain, within unincorporated Palm Beach County.

The proposed annexation meets the criteria for voluntary annexation, pursuant to Chapter 171, Sections 171.043 and 171.044, Florida Statutes. In addition, on October 23, 2025, the Palm Beach County Department of Planning, Zoning & Building issued a letter indicating that, after reviewing the proposed annexation, it had not identified any inconsistencies with Chapter 171, Florida Statutes.

Ordinance No. 5175-26 - Future Land Use Map Designation Assignment Upon annexation into the City, the property must be assigned a City FLU designation.

The County’s current FLU designation of Medium Residential-5 (MR-5) allows residential uses, including multifamily. Since the applicant is seeking to build 318 multifamily residential units on the site, the applicant is requesting a Multifamily (MF) FLU designation, which appears appropriate for this type of proposal. Attachment V includes maps showing the current and proposed FLU designation for the subject site and the current FLU designations for adjacent parcels.

Pursuant to Chapter 163 of the Florida Statutes, each level of service (LOS) element (i.e. drainage, potable water, wastewater, solid waste, school, and traffic) for any proposed FLU Map amendment shall be reviewed and analyzed by the City to determine if the new FLU designation, utilizing the maximum development potential, would cause any increase in impact, and if so, whether or not each public facility has the reserved capacity to accommodate the additional demand. The applicant prepared an LOS table that compares the existing and proposed FLU designations and their respective maximum development potential for the subject property. The table indicates an increase in water, wastewater, solid waste, and traffic impacts based on the additional density from the proposed City FLU designation as compared to the existing Palm Beach County FLU designation. The table shows that these LOS increases do not exceed the service provider capacities. Planning staff has confirmed the analysis.

The proposed assignment of the MF FLU designation meets two (2) of the Future Land Use Amendment Standards found in Future Land Use Element Policy 1.1.5 of the City’s Comprehensive Plan: Changed Assumptions and New Issues.

Ordinance No. 5176-26 - Zoning Map Designation Assignment Upon annexation into the City and the assignment of a City MF FLU designation, the property must be assigned a City zoning designation.

Multifamily high-density (MF32) is an appropriate zoning designation within an MF FLU designation.

The proposed assignment of the MF32 zoning designation complies with all eight (8) Rezoning Standards found in Section 94-32 of the City’s Zoning and Land Development Regulations.

WAIVER REQUEST

The applicant is seeking a 22.5% parking waiver, or 139 spaces, from the parking requirements outlined in Section 94-486, Table XV-6, City vehicular parking minimum requirements. Section 94-552.g.4 of the AWHO allows for this type of parking waiver provided the development is required to provide a series of mitigation strategies.

The Applicant is providing a variety of parking, mobility, and TDM strategies as measures to mitigate the need for personal vehicular use, such as the following:
- Rideshare Pick-Up Area Employer-Based Commuter Benefits Palm Tran Transit Subsidy for Residents of Workforce Units Construction of Palm Tran Shelter along Haverhill Road Bicycle Repair Station Bicycle Rack Shade Additional Bicycle Parking Free Indoor Bicycle Storage Designation of a Mobility Coordinator Unbundled Parking (so that parking spaces will not be tied to individual residential leases and will be unassigned to units)

The specific parking, mobility, and TDM requirements to be provided by this development are outlined in the conditions of approval outlined in Section II. RECOMMENDATION of the staff report and in Resolution No. 136-26.
