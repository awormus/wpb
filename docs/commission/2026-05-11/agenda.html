Griffin & Strong.

Background Information

In 2025, the City of West Palm Beach contracted with Griffin & Strong to perform a comprehensive Disparity Study to determine the effectiveness of the City's equal opportunity programs and whether a disparity continues to exist between the percentage of available Minority and Women-owned firms (referred to as “MBE” and “WBE” respectively or collectively, “MWBE”) within the market and the percentage of these firms utilized by the City in its procurement process.

Further, the purpose of this project was to determine if there continues to be a strong basis in evidence showing that willing and able Minority and Women-owned businesses are significantly underutilized in Construction, Professional Services (CCNA), Professional Services (Non-CCNA), Other Services, and Goods contracts awarded by the City, and if so, the extent to which such disparities may be attributed to discrimination.

Staff recommends that the commission receive and file the Disparity Study.

Background Information

Pursuant to the Florida Contraband Forfeiture Act, when a law enforcement agency obtains title to the forfeited property, the agency shall use the funds to pay for court costs incurred in the forfeiture proceedings. After that, the remaining funds shall be used for school resource officers, crime prevention, safe neighborhood, drug abuse education and prevention programs, or other law enforcement purposes, which include defraying the cost of protracted or complex investigations, providing additional equipment or expertise, purchasing automated external defibrillators for use in law enforcement vehicles, and providing matching funds to obtain federal grants. These funds may be expended upon request by the Chief of Police to the City Commission and upon appropriation to the police departments’ miscellaneous trust fund as the municipality’s governing body deems appropriate.

At the direction of the Chief of Police, the West Palm Beach Police Department (WPBPD) is seeking approval for the allocation of State Law Enforcement Trust funds for the following law enforcement equipment: GENASYS LRAD-450XL ($32,823): The WPBPD needs to replace the end-of-life Long Range Acoustic Device (LRAD). The current LRAD is no longer operational and non-serviceable. The selected replacement LRAD is the Genasys LRAD-450XL. The LRAD 450-XL will significantly enhance operational effectiveness and safety during high-risk search warrants, hostage barricade situations, hostage rescue operations, and large-scale crowd management events. SWAT operations frequently occur in densely populated residential and urban environments where clear, authoritative communication is essential. Traditional public address systems are often inadequate due to distance, structural barriers, or high ambient noise. The LRAD provides focused, intelligible voice projection that ensures lawful commands are heard and understood.

City Commission - May 11 2026

SMITH & WESSON – SIMUNITION M&P9 M2.0 TRAINING PISTOL KITSIMUNITION FX9004 TRAINING HELMETS and BLUE CANS ($32,716): The WPBPD SWAT Team is seeking to implement the listed equipment into the SWAT training environment.

The simulation equipment allows for a safe, realistic training experience. Simulation conversion kits and blue guns allow law enforcement to train with their actual service weapons using non-lethal, marking, or blank cartridges.

These tools enhance safety during force-on-force drills, increase realism by simulating weapon handling and recoil, improve decision-making under stress, and build muscle memory. The training helmet works in tandem with the simulation blue gun kit by providing personal protection to the cranial area of the training participant. The Blue Cans are nonfunctional training suppressors that replace an operator’s functioning long gun suppressor. The Blue Cans are utilized in the training environment to replicate the functionality and specifications of the operator’s long gun.

EXFIL BALLISTIC SL HELMET W/ BALLISTIC VISOR ($5,200): The WPBPD Bomb Unit is seeking to replace three (3) expired end-of-life ballistic helmets, including ballistic visors. Ballistic helmets and visors are critical, life-saving equipment for Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) personnel, protecting against fragmentation, overpressure, heat, and blunt impact from blasts. EOD helmets are designed to withstand highvelocity shrapnel and fragmentation from IEDs, which are primary threats during disposal operations. Preventing Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) is a primary function of EOD ballistic helmets by absorbing and dissipating impact energy, reducing the risk of concussions and TBIs. Ballistic visors offer critical protection for the face, covering the area from the helmet brim to below the chin against flying debris.

These specialized, lightweight, and often NIJ-rated systems protect the head and face, while allowing for essential integration of communications and situational awareness tools.

Total Request: $70,739.

Fiscal Note

Approval of this item will provide appropriations in the amount of $70,739 from State Law Enforcement Forfeiture Receipts for law enforcement equipment.

49th Street is a residential roadway that serves as a connector between North Flagler Drive and Greenwood Avenue and provides access to St. Mary’s Medical Center. The segment of 49th Street between Pinewood Avenue and Broadway Avenue currently lacks sidewalks and ADAaccessible facilities, resulting in residents and children walking along a heavily traveled roadway with limited separation from vehicular traffic.

In 2018, this segment of 49th Street was identified in the City of West Palm Beach Citywide Bicycle Master Plan as a recommended Bike Boulevard. Based on this designation, the City identified 49th Street as a suitable candidate for a Transportation Alternatives (TA) Grant. TA Grants are administered by the Palm Beach Transportation Planning Agency (TPA) and are intended to fund projects that improve safety and

Originating Department

Ordinance/Resolution

RESOLUTION NO. 98-26: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, VACATING AND ABANDONING A 13,645 SQUARE FOOT ALLEYWAY BETWEEN NORTH RAILROAD AVENUE, 10TH STREET, NORTH DIXIE HIGHWAY, AND 11TH STREET, AS ASSOCIATED WITH THE NORA PHASE 3 RESIDENTIAL MULTI-FAMILY DEVELOPMENTS; AUTHORIZING THE MAYOR TO EXECUTE A DISCLAIMER OF INTEREST IN THE AFORESAID ALLEYWAY; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

Background Information

Nora West 10-11 Owner, LLC and Nora East 10-11 Owner, LLC have proposed and received Downtown Action Committee (DAC) approval on April 8, 2026, for a new 11-story mixed-use residential development totaling approximately 476,003 gross square feet with 350 units and 16,819 square feet of ground floor commercial space at 456 11th Street.

Additionally, the NORA development team has an 11-story condominium project currently in for site plan review, which is also located on the same block.

The two (2) proposed development sites are currently bisected by a 14foot-wide 'T-shaped' alleyway located between 10th Street, North Railroad Avenue, 11th Street, and North Dixie Highway. The total square footage requested to be abandoned is approximately 13,645 (0.31 acres).

Subsequent to the abandonment, the development team is proposing to extend Madeira Court through the 10th to 11th Street block. This extension will serve as a private-access drive for the two (2) projects and will internalize their parking, loading, and sanitation services. A public access easement will be overlayed on this new drive/roadway once built.

In accordance with the Code of Ordinances, Planning staff asserts that the alleyway is not needed for any public purpose and the abandonment will comply with the criteria set forth in Section 78-217 of the Code of Ordinances.

City Commission - May 11 2026

Resolution No. 98-26 vacates and abandons the 13,645 square feet of right-of-way upon receipt of a revised appraisal fee of $680,000 as compensation for the value of the right-of-way. This value reflects the appraisal conducted by Anderson & Carr, Inc. at $800,000 and includes a 15% discount, as allowed by Code.

CONCLUSION: It is staff’s professional opinion that the alleyway subject to this abandonment is no longer needed by the City for any public purpose and that its removal will facilitate the applicant's proposed and approved NORA development.

PUBLIC NOTICE: In accordance with Section 78-215 of the City's Code of Ordinances, individual notices were mailed to all property owners within 500 feet of the property, and signs were posted on the subject alley right-of-way.
