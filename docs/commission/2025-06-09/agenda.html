The City’s Parks and Recreation Department, in conjunction with the Palm Beach County Early Learning Coalition (the “Coalition”) and the Children’s Services Council (“CSC”), provides childcare services for school-age children at the City’s two (2) licensed facilities: Pleasant City and South Olive Community Centers. Originating Department

THORNTON NURSERIES, INC.; WAIVING THE PROCUREMENT REQUIREMENTS PURSUANT TO SECTION 66-94 OF THE CITY CODE OF ORDINANCES; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

Background Information

On April 14, 2025, Resolution No. 106-25(F) was approved by the City Commission to amend the general fund budget for the relocation of trees in Dreher Park. The City wishes to relocate ten (10) large specimen trees, planted by Paul Dreher.

Pursuant to a ground lease agreement approved by the City Commission, the Boys & Girls Club is developing a club facility within Dreher Park. The City would like to enter into an agreement with the contractor who will be on-site planting and moving large trees for the Boys and Girls Club.

The contractor has the tree removal scheduled in the next few weeks. To facilitate the survival of relocated trees, there is insufficient time for the City to go through the formal procurement solicitation process to obtain a contractor to transplant large canopy trees.

The City believes economic efficiencies can be achieved by utilizing the same contractor to remove and relocate the ten (10) trees within Dreher Park. J.D. Thornton Nurseries, Inc. provided a proposal to transplant the trees at a cost of $108,910.

The City wishes to engage J.D. Thornton Nurseries, Inc. to relocate the ten (10) trees within Dreher Park. Pursuant to Section 66-94 of the Procurement Code, the Commission must approve contracts in excess of $50,000 that have not been competitively selected.

Resolution No. 128-25 waives the requirement for the procurement solicitation process and approves the agreement with J.D. Thornton Nurseries, Inc. to relocate the trees.

Public Information Officer (PIO) Equipment/Software ($12,248) The Public Information Officer (PIO) is crucial in law enforcement because they act as the main point of contact between the agency and the public, media, and other stakeholders. They ensure accurate and timely information dissemination, build trust, and shape public perception, especially during crises. Effective PIOs also manage social media, monitor public sentiment, and engage with communities to foster positive relationships. PIOs are also responsible for supporting the Incident Command System and the Emergency Operations Center.

PIOs establish and maintain positive relationships with the media, ensuring accurate and timely information dissemination. They shape narratives, manage sensitive data, and deliver clear and reassuring messages, especially during crises.

PIOs are adept at navigating the digital landscape, utilizing social media to reach broader audiences and engage effectively with the public. PIOs help agencies demonstrate transparency by sharing information about crime, department policies, and procedures. They verify the accuracy of all information before release and monitor media reports for accuracy, correcting any misinformation. PIOs also address communication to special needs and non-English speaking populations. PIOs can improve internal communication within the agency, fostering better collaboration and understanding between different departments. They can also help engage the community to guide policy and strategy, and gather data to support budgetary requests. In essence, the PIO is a multifaceted role that plays a vital part in law enforcement's ability to engage with the public, manage crises, and build trust.

Background Information

The City of West Palm Beach has prioritized the revitalization of Currie Park as part of its broader strategy to enhance public spaces, promote community engagement, and stimulate economic development along the City’s waterfront. Located in the Currie Corridor of the Community Redevelopment Agency (CRA), the 13-acre waterfront park is currently approved and funded for $35.3 million in renovations. Renovations include flood and stormwater mitigation work, the inclusion of a tidal amphitheater, a waterfront lagoon, a beach area, and other shoreline restoration and access infrastructure. Additional enhancements will include a playground, pedestrian/bicyclist trails, fitness lawn, and other community recreation and resilience facilities and features.

To support this transformative initiative, the Housing and Community Development Department (HCD) has allocated $1,287,996 in Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) funding to the Currie Park Redevelopment Project. These funds were originally appropriated in the City’s Community Development Fund. Governmental Accounting Standard Board (GASB) Statement Number 54 restricts the use of Special Revenue Funds to account for revenue and expenditures for purposes other than capital projects. It is acceptable for Special Revenue Fund 110, the Community Development Fund, to provide funds for eligible capital improvement projects. However, capital purchases cannot be expensed in Fund 110 per government accounting requirements.

Therefore, it is required that funds be transferred out from Fund 110 to the Grant Capital Project Fund 399 so the capital payments can be expensed and reported properly in financial statements.

Resolution No. 162-23, Resolution No. 73-25, and Resolution No. 9525(F) amended the Annual One-Year Action Plans for Program Years 2020 through 2025 for the CDBG, to reallocate funding to eligible public facilities/improvement projects. A portion of these funds will be used for the Currie Park Redevelopment project.

On April 7, 2025, HCD entered into an Inter-Department Subrecipient Agreement with the Engineering Department for the use of CDBG funds for the Currie Park Redevelopment project. The project is eligible under the CDBG program regulations, as per 24 CFR 570.210(c), and meets the National Objective of Area Benefit Activities, as defined by 24 CFR 570.208(a)(1).

Resolution No. 135-25(F) approves the transfer of the grant funds.
