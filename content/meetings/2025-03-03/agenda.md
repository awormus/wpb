## 4. CIVILITY AND DECORUM

The City of West Palm Beach is committed to civility and decorum by its officials, employees and members of the public who attend this meeting. The City Code, Secs. 2-31(8), 2-31(18) and 2-31(22), provides in pertinent part:
- Officials shall be recognized by the Chair and shall not interrupt a speaker.
- Public comment shall be addressed to the City Commission as a whole and not to any individual on the dais or in the audience.
- Displays of anger, rudeness, ridicule, impatience, lack of respect and personal attacks are strictly prohibited.
- Unauthorized remarks from the audience, stamping of feet, whistles, yells and similar demonstrations shall not be permitted.
- Offenders may be removed from the meeting.
- Any person desiring to address the Commission shall file a written request with the city clerk prior to consideration of the matter by the Commission or prior to the public comment portion of a meeting. The person wishing to speak shall complete a comment card for each agenda item the person wishes to address, which shall include the person's full name, address, and the numbered agenda item. The person will not be recognized if the comment card is not completed.

#### Background Information

In February 2024, the West Palm Beach Police Department (WPBPD) submitted a concept paper to the Florida Department of Transportation (FDOT) regarding the proposed use of grant funds to be included in the State’s Fiscal Year 2025 Highway Safety Plan. The paper used statistics on crash outcomes in the City of West Palm Beach and the rest of the State to help justify why this education and enforcement plan targeting occupant restraint usage, safety belts, and child restraint devices should be funded in the City of West Palm Beach.

Safety belts and child restraints, when used properly, are one of the most effective ways to reduce injuries and save lives during crashes. The City of West Palm Beach is located in Palm Beach County and is the largest city by population and square miles in the county. The population is currently 115,000 and has been increasing at about 4.5% yearly since 2019. According to the 2024 FDOT safety belt usage report for the State of Florida, the safety belt usage rate for 2024 is 90.0%. This rate is up 0.6% points from the 2023 measured rate of 89.4%. The report found that usage on major roadways such as highways/interstates was measured higher at 92.2% while on smaller arterial and local roads measured at 90.6 % and 86.7%, respectively.

Of the over 115,000 residents of the City of West Palm Beach, 33% are African American (U.S. Census Bureau), and this community safety belt usage average is below that of White, Hispanic, and “Other” by 6-10% (FDOT 2024 safety belt usage survey).

The West Palm Beach Police Department (WPBPD) integrates high


City Commission - Mar 03 2025

visibility enforcement, community outreach, and publicity to educate the public on safety belt usage. As outlined in the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) Countermeasures that Work, 11th Edition: 3-15, Short-Term, High Visibility Seat Belt Law Enforcement; 3-16, ShortTerm, High Visibility Child Passenger Safety Law Enforcement; 3-19, Nighttime, High Visibility Seat Belt Law Enforcement, and 3-22, Other Strategies for Behavior Change guidelines, the West Palm Beach Police Department will continue with the high visibility enforcement details and educational outreach. The department has adjusted the enforcement to the needs of the community. We are using crash data to determine where the most crashes are occurring and focusing our enforcement in those areas. Sub-grant funds will be used for the purchase of traffic cones and a trailer for their deployment in order to block lanes to conduct safety belt checkpoints. The cones will help officers conduct the checkpoint operations safely.

The WPBPD utilizes our Public Information Officer (PIO) to publicize our enforcement efforts through local news outlets and social media. We will increase our enforcement efforts in school zones to increase awareness of the importance of safety belt usage for kids. We will also continue to be part of the events held throughout the community to help educate and raise awareness about the importance of safety belt usage. The WPBPD is an active participant in all Florida Department of Transportationsponsored enforcement waves, such as the national Click it or Ticket campaign, and will continue to participate in the upcoming years.

The West Palm Beach Police Department was awarded a grant from the Florida Department of Transportation in the amount of $111,000 to fund the Police Department's Occupant Protection Program (seatbelts and child restraint device usage).

Resolution No. 43-25 accepts the grant and authorizes the execution of all grant documents.

#### Fiscal Note

The CWPB hosts many large-scale events, including The Marathon of the Palm Beaches, Palm Beach Boat Show, Sunfest, 4th on Flager, and a dozen other races/walks and outside events downtown.
