## 4. CIVILITY AND DECORUM

      The City of West Palm Beach is committed to civility and decorum by its officials, employees
      and members of the public who attend this meeting. The City Code, Secs. 2-31(8), 2-31(18)
      and 2-31(22), provides in pertinent part:
         - Officials shall be recognized by the Chair and shall not interrupt a speaker.
         - Public comment shall be addressed to the City Commission as a whole and not to any
            individual on the dais or in the audience.
         - Displays of anger, rudeness, ridicule, impatience, lack of respect and personal attacks
            are strictly prohibited.
         - Unauthorized remarks from the audience, stamping of feet, whistles, yells and similar
            demonstrations shall not be permitted.
         - Offenders may be removed from the meeting.
         - Any person desiring to address the Commission shall file a written request with the city
            clerk prior to consideration of the matter by the Commission or prior to the public
            comment portion of a meeting. The person wishing to speak shall complete a comment
            card for each agenda item the person wishes to address, which shall include the
            person's full name, address, and the numbered agenda item. The person will not be
            recognized if the comment card is not completed.

            Background Information:
            In 2025, the City of West Palm Beach contracted with Griffin & Strong to

           MISCELLANEOUS       TRUST   FUND  BUDGET     TO   PROVIDE
           APPROPRIATIONS FOR LAW ENFORCEMENT EQUIPMENT TO
           INCLUDE A LONG RANGE ACOUSTIC DEVICE SYSTEM, SIMUNITION
           TRAINING EQUIPMENT AND BALLISTIC EQUIPMENT; PROVIDING
           AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.
           Background Information:
           Pursuant to the Florida Contraband Forfeiture Act, when a law
           enforcement agency obtains title to the forfeited property, the agency
           shall use the funds to pay for court costs incurred in the forfeiture
           proceedings. After that, the remaining funds shall be used for school
           resource officers, crime prevention, safe neighborhood, drug abuse
           education and prevention programs, or other law enforcement purposes,
           which include defraying the cost of protracted or complex investigations,
           providing additional equipment or expertise, purchasing automated
           external defibrillators for use in law enforcement vehicles, and providing
           matching funds to obtain federal grants. These funds may be expended
           upon request by the Chief of Police to the City Commission and upon
           appropriation to the police departments’ miscellaneous trust fund as the
           municipality’s governing body deems appropriate.

           At the direction of the Chief of Police, the West Palm Beach Police
           Department (WPBPD) is seeking approval for the allocation of State Law
           Enforcement Trust funds for the following law enforcement equipment:

           GENASYS LRAD-450XL ($32,823): The WPBPD needs to replace the
           end-of-life Long Range Acoustic Device (LRAD). The current LRAD is no
           longer operational and non-serviceable. The selected replacement LRAD
           is the Genasys LRAD-450XL. The LRAD 450-XL will significantly
           enhance operational effectiveness and safety during high-risk search
           warrants, hostage barricade situations, hostage rescue operations, and
           large-scale crowd management events. SWAT operations frequently
           occur in densely populated residential and urban environments where
           clear, authoritative communication is essential. Traditional public address
           systems are often inadequate due to distance, structural barriers, or high
           ambient noise. The LRAD provides focused, intelligible voice projection
           that ensures lawful commands are heard and understood.


City Commission - May 11 2026


           SMITH & WESSON – SIMUNITION M&P9 M2.0 TRAINING PISTOL KIT-
           SIMUNITION FX9004 TRAINING HELMETS and BLUE CANS
           ($32,716): The WPBPD SWAT Team is seeking to implement the listed
           equipment into the SWAT training environment.                The simulation
           equipment allows for a safe, realistic training experience. Simulation
           conversion kits and blue guns allow law enforcement to train with their
           actual service weapons using non-lethal, marking, or blank cartridges.
           These tools enhance safety during force-on-force drills, increase realism
           by simulating weapon handling and recoil, improve decision-making
           under stress, and build muscle memory. The training helmet works in
           tandem with the simulation blue gun kit by providing personal protection
           to the cranial area of the training participant. The Blue Cans are non-
           functional training suppressors that replace an operator’s functioning long
           gun suppressor. The Blue Cans are utilized in the training environment to
           replicate the functionality and specifications of the operator’s long gun.

           EXFIL BALLISTIC SL HELMET W/ BALLISTIC VISOR ($5,200): The
           WPBPD Bomb Unit is seeking to replace three (3) expired end-of-life
           ballistic helmets, including ballistic visors. Ballistic helmets and visors are
           critical, life-saving equipment for Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD)
           personnel, protecting against fragmentation, overpressure, heat, and
           blunt impact from blasts. EOD helmets are designed to withstand high-
           velocity shrapnel and fragmentation from IEDs, which are primary threats
           during disposal operations. Preventing Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) is a
           primary function of EOD ballistic helmets by absorbing and dissipating
           impact energy, reducing the risk of concussions and TBIs. Ballistic visors
           offer critical protection for the face, covering the area from the helmet
           brim to below the chin against flying debris.               These specialized,
           lightweight, and often NIJ-rated systems protect the head and face, while
           allowing for essential integration of communications and situational
           awareness tools.

           Total Request: $70,739.

           accessible facilities, resulting in residents and children walking along a

           In 2018, this segment of 49th Street was identified in the City of West
           Palm Beach Citywide Bicycle Master Plan as a recommended Bike
           Boulevard. Based on this designation, the City identified 49th Street as a
           suitable candidate for a Transportation Alternatives (TA) Grant. TA
           Grants are administered by the Palm Beach Transportation Planning
           Agency (TPA) and are intended to fund projects that improve safety and

           Project). The proposed Project includes the replacement of existing
           sidewalks with ADA-compliant sidewalks and ramps, installation of traffic-
           calming devices, and bicycle safety enhancements. The TPA awarded
           the grant to the City and included the Project on its Priority Project List for
           Fiscal Years 2024 through 2028.

City Commission - May 11 2026


            Resolution No. 98-26 vacates and abandons the 13,645 square feet of
            right-of-way upon receipt of a revised appraisal fee of $680,000 as
            compensation for the value of the right-of-way. This value reflects the
            appraisal conducted by Anderson & Carr, Inc. at $800,000 and includes a
            15% discount, as allowed by Code.

            CONCLUSION: It is staff’s professional opinion that the alleyway subject
            to this abandonment is no longer needed by the City for any public
            purpose and that its removal will facilitate the applicant's proposed and
            approved NORA development.

            PUBLIC NOTICE: In accordance with Section 78-215 of the City's Code
            of Ordinances, individual notices were mailed to all property owners
            within 500 feet of the property, and signs were posted on the subject alley
            right-of-way.

            Commission District 3: Commissioner Christy Fox.

            Fiscal Note:
            City to receive $680,000.
