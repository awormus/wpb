## 4. CIVILITY AND DECORUM

      The City of West Palm Beach is committed to civility and decorum by its officials, employees
      and members of the public who attend this meeting. The City Code, Secs. 2-31(8), 2-31(18)
      and 2-31(22), provides in pertinent part:
         - Officials shall be recognized by the Chair and shall not interrupt a speaker.
         - Public comment shall be addressed to the City Commission as a whole and not to any
            individual on the dais or in the audience.
         - Displays of anger, rudeness, ridicule, impatience, lack of respect and personal attacks
            are strictly prohibited.
         - Unauthorized remarks from the audience, stamping of feet, whistles, yells and similar
            demonstrations shall not be permitted.
         - Offenders may be removed from the meeting.
         - Any person desiring to address the Commission shall file a written request with the city
            clerk prior to consideration of the matter by the Commission or prior to the public
            comment portion of a meeting. The person wishing to speak shall complete a comment
            card for each agenda item the person wishes to address, which shall include the
            person's full name, address, and the numbered agenda item. The person will not be
            recognized if the comment card is not completed.

### 7.4. Resolution No. 67-26(F) approving the transfer of $206,197 of City funds

           in Fiscal Year 2025/2026 for the purpose of amending the Grants Capital
           Project fund budget to provide additional appropriations for the Currie
           Park Redevelopment project.
           Originating Department:
           Housing and Community Development
           Ordinance/Resolution:
           RESOLUTION NO. 67-26(F): A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY
           COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA,
           AUTHORIZING THE APPROPRIATION OR TRANSFER OF CITY
           FUNDS IN FISCAL YEAR 2025/2026 IN ACCORDANCE WITH
           SECTION 4.03 OF THE CITY CHARTER OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM
           BEACH, FLORIDA, FOR THE PURPOSE OF AMENDING THE
           COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT FUND AND GRANT CAPITAL PROJECT
           FUND BUDGETS TO PROVIDE FOR APPROPRIATIONS FOR THE
           CURRIE PARK REDEVELOPMENT PROJECT; PROVIDING AN
           EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.
           Background Information:
           The City of West Palm Beach has prioritized the revitalization of Currie
           Park as part of its broader strategy to enhance public spaces, promote
           community engagement, and stimulate economic development along the
           City’s waterfront. Located in the Currie Corridor of the Community
           Redevelopment Agency (CRA), the 13-acre waterfront park is currently
           approved and funded for $35.3 million in renovations. Renovations
           include: flood and stormwater mitigation work, the inclusion of a tidal
           amphitheater, a waterfront lagoon, a beach area, and other shoreline
           restoration and access infrastructure. Additional enhancements will
           include a playground, pedestrian/bicyclist trails, fitness lawn, and other
           community recreation and resilience facilities and features.

           To support this transformative initiative, by Resolution No. 135-25(F),
           approved by City Commission on June 9, 2025, the Housing and
           Community Development Department (HCD) allocated $1,287,996 in
           Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) funding to the Currie Park
           Redevelopment Project. HCD desires to allocate additional funding of
           $206,197 in CDBG funding to the project. These funds were originally
           appropriated in the City’s Community Development Fund. Governmental
           Accounting Standard Board (GASB) Statement Number 54 restricts the
           use of Special Revenue Funds to account for revenue and expenditures
           for purposes other than capital projects. It is acceptable for Special
           Revenue Fund 110, the Community Development Fund, to provide funds
           for eligible capital improvement projects. However, capital purchases
           cannot be expensed in Fund 110, per government accounting
           requirements. Therefore, it is required that funds be transferred out from
           Fund 110 to the Grant Capital Project Fund 399, so the capital payments
           can be expensed and reported properly in financial statements.

City Commission - Mar 16 2026


           Resolution No. 162-23, Resolution No. 73-25, and Resolution No. 95-
           25(F) amended the Annual One-Year Action Plans for Program Years
           2020 through 2025 for the CDBG to reallocate funding to eligible public
           facilities/improvement projects. A portion of these funds will be used for
           the Currie Park Redevelopment project.

           On April 7, 2025, HCD entered into an Inter-Department Subrecipient
           Agreement with the Engineering Department for the use of CDBG funds
           for the Currie Park Redevelopment project. The Inter-Department
           Subrecipient Agreement is being amended to increase the allocation by
           $206,197. The project is eligible under the CDBG program regulations,
           as per 24 CFR 570.210(c), and meets the National Objective of Area
           Benefit Activities, as defined by 24 CFR 570.208(a)(1).

           Resolution No. 67-26(F) approves the transfer of the grant funds.

           Commission District 1: Commissioner Cathleen Ward.

           Approval authorizes the transfer of funds from CDBG Fund 110 to the
           Grant Capital Project Fund 399.

           Background Information:
           The State of Florida, Department of Financial Services, approved the
           Florida Firefighter Technical Equipment Grant application and awarded
           the City of West Palm Beach Fire Department a $254,766 grant for the
           purchase of dive rescue-related equipment. Some examples of dive
           equipment to be purchased include: Viking Haztech Drysuits, OTS MK-III
           Diver Communication Systems, Gemini Switch Blocks, Surface Support
           Equipment, and ZOLL Monitors.

           The purchase of the equipment will keep the firefighters safe from
           contaminated water, enable communication in the most challenging
           underwater environments, and ensure divers' safety and uninterrupted air
           supply during emergency situations. It will also provide rapid, accurate
           detection of hazardous substances, including toxic chemicals, explosives,
           and narcotics. The Zoll EKG monitors will provide the latest technology to
           assist in monitoring a patient’s cardiac rhythm and deliver defibrillation for
           life-threatening arrhythmias. Zoll monitors are a vital component of
           patient assessment and will be used on all patients during cardiac arrest,
           including patients seen by paramedics.

           Additionally, the purchase of dive equipment will improve emergency
           response time by several minutes and ensure more equipped divers are
           spread throughout the City. It will allow divers to deploy life-saving
           measures more rapidly and will have a significant impact on the response
           to high-profile events and locations such as Mar-a-Lago, the convention
           center, and other venues.

           As a condition of the grant, the City of West Palm Beach is required to
           contribute $382,150 in addition to accepting the grant award, for a total
           budget of $636,916.

           Background Information:
           The City of West Palm Beach is the owner of certain real property located
           at 202, 204, and 206 North Sapodilla, consisting of approximately 0.79

           By Resolution No. 272-25, the City Commission declared the property to

           authorized negotiations with Publix Super Markets, Inc. (“Publix”), for its

           Publix desires to relocate its current supermarket within the West Palm
           Beach downtown to the Property to allow development of a more
           expansive store. Publix is under contract to purchase the adjacent
           properties, which comprise the remainder of the block.

           A Phase I environmental report indicated the existence of several
           contaminants on the Property resulting from the Property’s prior uses,
           which included a railroad spur, a manufactured gas plant, and a coal
           storage area.

           City staff and Publix have negotiated the sale of the Property subject to
           the following key terms and conditions:

               Purchase Price: The purchase price of Three Million Five Hundred
               Thousand Six Hundred Eighty-Four Dollars ($3,526,684) represents
               85% of the appraised values, adjusted for environmental remediation,
               in compliance with Section 2-31(27)(c) of the Code of Ordinances.

               Supermarket Use Restriction: Publix intends to construct and
               operate a full-service supermarket of a minimum of 40,000 square
               feet, offering a wide variety of groceries, including fresh produce,
               meats, dairy, beer and wine, pantry staples, plus household goods
               and non-food items, including a pharmacy, with associated parking.
               A restrictive covenant shall require the Property to be used for a
               supermarket for a term of fifteen (15) years. Other uses will be
               allowed on the Property so long as the supermarket remains in
               operation.
