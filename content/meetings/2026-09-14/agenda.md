#### Originating Department

> RESOLUTION NO. 185-26: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, APPROVING AN INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH AND PALM BEACH COUNTY FOR PARTICIPATION IN THE 2026-2029 DROWNING PREVENTION COALITION’S LEARN TO SWIM PROGRAM; PROVIDING FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

#### Background Information

Palm Beach County Fire Rescue manages and houses the Drowning Prevention Coalition (DPC). Palm Beach County, through the DPC, operates the “DPC Learn to Swim Program”. In this program, the DPC distributes vouchers to the public that can be redeemed for swimming lessons at designated aquatic facilities.

The DPC requests the City of West Palm Beach Department of Parks and Recreation to be a designated facility and provide Learn to Swim lessons using voucher reimbursement. Under this agreement, the City would provide Learn to Swim lessons at Warren Hawkins Aquatic Center to voucher recipients. The Learn to Swim lessons provided would be paid up to $60 per voucher by the DPC.

The City has been a partner with the DPC since 2006 and would like to renew the agreement for free or reduced-cost lessons, recognizing the importance of knowing how to swim in Florida, with canals, lakes, ponds, and beaches.

Resolution No. 185-26 approves the Interlocal Agreement with Palm Beach County providing Learn to Swim vouchers for lessons offered at the Warren Hawkins Aquatic Center in Gaines Park.

Resolution No. 194-26(F) accepting and appropriating funds in the amount of $4,250 from Prime Time of Palm Beach County, funded by the Children Services Council, in recognition of the after-school programs at Gaines Park, Pleasant City, and South Olive Park, completing the Quality Improvement System (QIS) core quality practices.

#### Originating Department

Parks and Recreation

#### Ordinance/Resolution


> RESOLUTION NO. 194-26(F): A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, AUTHORIZING THE APPROPRIATION OR TRANSFER OF CITY FUNDS IN FISCAL YEAR 2025/2026 IN ACCORDANCE WITH SECTION 4.03 OF THE CITY CHARTER OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, FOR THE PURPOSE OF AMENDING THE DONATION FUND TO PROVIDE FOR THE RECEIPT AND APPROPRIATION OF THE GRANT AWARD FROM PRIME TIME OF PALM BEACH COUNTY TO PROVIDE AFTER SCHOOL PROGRAM SUPPLIES, PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT, FIELD TRIPS, SPECIAL YOUTH/FAMILY EVENTS, OTHER PROGRAM EXPENDITURES; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

#### Background Information

The Quality Improvement System (QIS) incentive program is a Prime Time of Palm Beach County strategy designed to further increase after-school program quality for its participants' activities/programs. The QIS is a performance-based recognition program that awards financial incentives to out-of-school time programs that go above and beyond in their QIS work.

The award amount is based upon a tiered process through which a program demonstrates the implementation of advanced youth development and program management practices. These practices are in concert with and directly relate to a program's voluntary participation and work in Prime Time's QIS incentive program. Funding is provided by the Children's Services Council of Palm Beach County and is based upon the availability of funds and performance.

This past year, three (3) of the City's after-school programs were eligible to receive a QIS Core Practices Development Grant through Prime Time's QIS incentive program. They have each completed the QIS core quality practices, which aim to increase and maintain positive youth development, helping young people develop the skills necessary to be successful socially, emotionally, and academically. Prime Time requires that the grant be spent on professional development, program supplies, field trips, special youth and/or family events, and other program expenditures.

Gaines Park Community Center, South Olive Community Center, and Pleasant City Community Center have each been recognized for going above and beyond in their QIS work. Pleasant City K-5th grade was awarded $1,500; Gaines Park 6th-8th grade was awarded $1,500; and South Olive K-5th grade was awarded $1,250.

Resolution No. 194-26(F) accepts and appropriates funds in the amount of $4,250 in recognition of the after-school programs at Gaines Park, Pleasant City, and South Olive Park, completing the Quality Improvement System (QIS) core quality practices.

#### Background Information

Florida's State Aid to Libraries Program is administered by the Florida Department of State, Division of Library and Information Services. In 2003, the Legislature revised the law to allow independent municipal libraries to participate in the State Aid to Libraries Program. This was done in recognition that the provision of State Aid to independent municipal libraries is a valuable contribution to the extension of the State's public library development plan. The State provides a match of up to 25 cents on each dollar of local funds expended centrally for the operation and maintenance of the library. The Mandel Public Library of West Palm Beach has received this State Aid funding since 2003.

The Mandel Public Library meets the following requirements to receive the
State Aid to Libraries Grant:
1. Provides free library services; 2. Maintains an established interlocal agreement to jointly support each participating library; 3. Retains a single administrative head employed full-time to manage or coordinate the operations of the library; 4. Expends funds centrally; 5. Maintains a reciprocal borrowing agreement; 6. Provides access to materials, information, and services for all residents of the area served; 7. Open forty (40) hours or more each week; and 8. Established a long-range plan, an annual plan of service, and an annual budget.

Funding from the State will supplement the Mandel Public Library's operating costs for goods and services as defined by the Uniform Accounting System Manual for Local Governments, 2023 edition, prepared by the Florida Department of Financial Services, Bureau of Financial Reporting.

Resolution No. 50-24 approved the art concept “Uplift” to be sited at Broadway and 24th Street for The District, but due to technical complications in integrating the artwork to the building, a new site was identified, and the artist created a new concept for the development site (1% escrow $925,000).

#### Originating Department

#### Ordinance/Resolution


> RESOLUTION NO. 203-26: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA, APPROVING THE PROPOSED PUBLIC ART ARTWORK “CROSSROADS” BY KAI FOR THE DISTRICT DEVELOPMENT PROJECT COMMISSIONED BY IMMOCORP, LLC, IN COMPLIANCE WITH THE PUBLIC ART ORDINANCE; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND OTHER PURPOSES.

#### Staff Recommended Motion

Approve Resolution No. 203-26.

This motion is based on the findings that the proposed artworks meet the criteria set forth in Section 43-12 of the City Code, as reviewed by the Art in Public Places Advisory Committee.

#### Background Information

On Wednesday August 5, 2026, at their regularly scheduled meeting, the Art in Public Places Advisory Committee voted unanimously to recommend Kai’s revised public art proposal to the City Commission for The District.

#### BACKGROUND OF ORIGINAL PROPOSAL

On February 7, 2024, at their regularly scheduled meeting, ArtLife voted unanimously to recommend "Uplift" by artist Kai to the City Commission.

Immocorp Ventures, LLC are the Developers who commissioned Los Angeles-based artist Kai to create a large-scale sculpture that would be integrated into one of the buildings. Architectural Technical challenges required the artist and Developer to revisit the original proposed concept.

A new site on the development warranted a new site-specific artwork.

#### ABOUT THE ARTIST

Los Angeles-based street artist, Kai, has created a signature character "Imaginary Friend" (IF) to build a story and send a message. This minimalist cartoon character was designed with no discernible race, gender, or status, aiming to symbolize social inclusivity. The artist utilizes IF in all his works: monumental sculptures, integrated into architecture, murals, and video.

Kai received his BFA from the California Institute of Arts and studied abroad at L'Ecole des Beaux Arts.

#### ABOUT THE WORK

The sculpture illustrates the artist’s signature visual language, showcasing IF, a character Kai conceived intentionally to be open to interpretation. IF is presented as a large, hemispherical dome and will be built from smooth, highly polished bronze. The highly reflective surface captures the surrounding architecture, sky, movement, and people of Northwood, allowing the neighborhood itself to become part of the artwork. The sculpture changes continuously as its environment changes. In this way, the work does not simply occupy Northwood, it reflects Northwood back to itself.

The material also creates a deliberate bridge between the artist’s style and the visual art language of blue-chip contemporary art. The monumental scale, playful character, and mirror-like surface evoke iconic works such as Jeff Koons’ Balloon Dog, while remaining rooted in his own visual vocabulary. Placed intentionally within an urban plaza, Crossroads becomes a prominent landmark and a point of arrival. #### ABOUT THE ARTWORK

The Bellini Tree reflects the Haas Brothers’ distinctive approach to transforming familiar elements of the natural world into fantastical, almost otherworldly organisms. Throughout their practice, twins Nikolai and Simon Haas explore the intersection of nature, fantasy, craft, design, and technology, creating biomorphic works that feel simultaneously familiar and impossible. Their sculptures invite viewers into an imagined ecosystem where the rules of nature are recognizable but deliberately altered.

This tension between the natural and the unreal is central to the Tree Series. The form begins with something universally recognizable, a tree, yet the artists intentionally disrupt expectations through exaggerated color, unexpected materials, intricate ornamentation, and impossible botanical details. The deep red glass fruit, for example, suggests a familiar natural form while transforming it into something mythical. The artwork appears to belong to nature, but it could never actually grow there. That sense of recognition followed by surprise is fundamental to the Haas Brothers’ visual language.

Their process is equally important. The Haas Brothers combine traditional craft with sophisticated fabrication and a highly collaborative studio practice, working across materials including bronze, beads, ceramics, wood, and blown glass. Their meticulous craftsmanship gives the fantastical forms a physical presence and complexity that makes them feel almost alive. The contrast between the precision of the making and the playful improbability of the finished object is a defining characteristic of their work.

In The Bellini Tree, the textured bronze trunk establishes the weight and permanence of a mature tree, while the hand-beaded vines, foliage, and luminous glass fruit introduce an element of wonder. The vibrant teal-cyan trunk and deep red botanical elements move the sculpture even further from the expected palette of nature. Rather than reproducing a tree, the Haas Brothers reinvent the idea of one, creating a hybrid organism that feels as though it has emerged from an alternate natural world.

The result is playful without being superficial. The Bellini Tree embodies the Haas Brothers’ ability to use humor, imagination, color, and extraordinary craftsmanship to encourage viewers to look at nature differently. It asks us to suspend what we know to be possible and enter a world where trees can become fantastical, luminous, and alive with their own mythology.

#### Staff Recommended Motion

Approve Ordinance No. 5182-26 on First Reading and schedule Second Reading for September 28, 2026.

#### Background Information

In 2024, the Florida Legislature, through House Bill 479, formally defined mobility plans and mobility fees and reaffirmed that local governments may adopt an alternative transportation system based on a mobility plan. House Bill 479 further provides that only one (1) transportation mitigation fee may be collected within a municipality and that the local government issuing building permits is responsible for collecting such fees.

In 2025, the City of West Palm Beach adopted a Downtown Mobility Fee as the first phase of transitioning away from Palm Beach County's transportation concurrency and road impact fee system. The proposed ordinance represents the next step in that transition by establishing a citywide Mobility Fee system based on the 2050 West Palm Beach Mobility Plan and integrating the existing Downtown Mobility Fee into a comprehensive, multimodal transportation funding framework.

The 2050 West Palm Beach Mobility Plan, dated August 2026, identifies the mobility projects needed to accommodate projected growth through the year 2050 and establishes a long-range strategy for enhancing the City's multimodal transportation system. The Plan consists of six elements: Streets, Intersections, Bicycle and Pedestrian Facilities, Transit, Future Corridors, and Mobility Implementation Projects. The mobility projects identified in the Plan support future growth while improving accessibility, connectivity, safety, and transportation choices for residents, businesses, employees, and visitors.

The City of West Palm Beach Mobility Fee Technical Report, dated August 2026, documents the data, methodology, and legal analysis used to develop the Mobility Fee. The Technical Report demonstrates that the Mobility Fee satisfies the legally established dual rational nexus requirements for "need" and "benefit," is roughly proportional to the impacts of new development, and is consistent with Florida Statutes Sections 163.3164, 163.3180, 163.31801, and Chapter 380, Florida Statutes.

The proposed Mobility Fee system establishes four (4) Mobility Fee Assessment Areas and corresponding Benefit Districts: Downtown, North, South, and West. Mobility fees assessed on new development will be deposited into special revenue funds and expended on mobility projects that provide a direct benefit to the development that paid the fee. The Mobility Fee will replace Palm Beach County road impact fees and transportation concurrency within the City's Mobility Fee Assessment Areas and will serve as the sole transportation mitigation fee assessed on new development within those areas.

Mobility Fees are not a recurring tax, are not assessed on existing residential or non-residential properties, and are not deposited into the City's General Fund. Rather, they are a one-time assessment on new development intended to offset the travel demand impacts associated with growth and to fund mobility improvements identified in the 2050 West Palm Beach Mobility Plan.

Adoption of the proposed ordinance will provide a predictable and transparent mechanism to fund transportation improvements needed to accommodate future growth while advancing the vision of "A West Palm Beach where getting around is simple and seamless." The Mobility Fee system supports a connected, multimodal transportation network and ensures that new development contributes its proportionate share toward the infrastructure necessary to support future residents, employees, and visitors.

#### Fiscal Note

Mobility fees that are collected will be utilized towards mobility-related improvements specifically identified in the mobility plan.
