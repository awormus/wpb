## 4. CIVILITY AND DECORUM

The City of West Palm Beach is committed to civility and decorum by its officials, employees
and members of the public who attend this meeting. The City Code, Secs. 2-31(8), 2-31(18)
and 2-31(22), provides in pertinent part:
- Officials shall be recognized by the Chair and shall not interrupt a speaker.
- Public comment shall be addressed to the City Commission as a whole and not to any
individual on the dais or in the audience.
- Displays of anger, rudeness, ridicule, impatience, lack of respect and personal attacks
are strictly prohibited.
- Unauthorized remarks from the audience, stamping of feet, whistles, yells and similar
demonstrations shall not be permitted.
- Offenders may be removed from the meeting.
- Any person desiring to address the Commission shall file a written request with the city
clerk prior to consideration of the matter by the Commission or prior to the public
comment portion of a meeting. The person wishing to speak shall complete a comment
card for each agenda item the person wishes to address, which shall include the
person's full name, address, and the numbered agenda item. The person will not be
recognized if the comment card is not completed.

### 7.5. Resolution No. 228-26 requests approval of a contract amendment with

Axon Enterprise, Inc., which will consolidate three (3) existing contracts into
one (1) eight-year agreement with an amount not to exceed $16,064,926.
Originating Department:
Police
Ordinance/Resolution:
RESOLUTION NO. 228-26: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA,
APPROVING AN AMENDMENT TO THE MASTER SERVICES AND
PURCHASING AGREEMENT BETWEEN AXON ENTERPRISE, INC.
AND THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH IN THE AMOUNT NOT TO
EXCEED $16,064,926 OVER A TERM OF EIGHT (8) YEARS FOR THE
PURCHASE OF VARIOUS EQUIPMENT, HARDWARE AND SOFTWARE
FOR THE WEST PALM BEACH POLICE DEPARTMENT; PROVIDING
FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.

Background Information:
Axon has been the West Palm Beach Police Department’s public safety
technology platform for over ten (10) years. Deployed agency-wide are
body-worn cameras, TASERs, in-car and interview room video, the Fusus
real-time crime center platform, unmanned aircraft with rooftop docks, and
Axon Evidence, the system of record for all video evidence. Officers are
trained on it, procedures are built around it, and more than thirty (30)
terabytes of evidence from active and closed cases reside in it.
That capability was acquired incrementally through five (5) contracts, each
with its own term and renewal date. The Department has also identified
nine (9) additional capabilities needed in coming years, including AIassisted report writing, live in-field translation, and video search and
transcription. These are functions of cameras and evidence infrastructure
the City already owns, not standalone products, and their pricing depends
on consolidation. The request adds no new vendor and replaces no
infrastructure.
IMPORTANT TO NOTE
1. Integration is an operational requirement. The new capabilities run
on equipment the Department already fields. Equivalents from other
vendors would require connections between competing systems
that either can discontinue mid-term. Within one platform,
integration is contractual.
2. Regional interoperability. The Palm Beach County Sheriff’s Office,
Riviera Beach Police Department, and Town of Palm Beach Police
Department all operate on Axon. Evidence moves between
agencies with no break in chain of custody, and sharing with the
State Attorney’s Office is free. Departing would leave West Palm
Beach the only major agency in its area outside it.
3. Financial analysis. The new consolidated contract will have a value
of $16,064,925. The Department also continues to pay two (2)
previously approved drone contracts through their 2030 expiration,
about $1,241,616 under existing authority, which then fold into this
agreement at no additional cost.

4. The Department has added thirty-six (36) sworn positions, each
requiring a body-worn camera and TASER. That $1,127,480 is a
committed obligation under every option, so it is carried in all three
(3) rather than treated as a choice. Option 1, taking no action and
renewing the five (5) contracts as they expire, costs $16,587,747.
Option 2, adding nine (9) new capabilities under a separate sixth
Axon contract, costs a total of $23,422,933. Option 3, the proposal,
costs $718,795, or 4.3 percent (4.3%), above taking no action, and
$6,116,391 below Option 2. Pricing is fixed rather than exposed to
future re-procurements, and vandalism coverage worth $113,720 is
added at no charge.
5. The eight-year term. This term derives from equipment lifecycle:
body-worn cameras and TASERs run on a five-year amortization
and refresh cycle, and the Department is two (2) years in with three
(3) remaining. Eight (8) years completes that cycle and delivers one
(1) full replacement cycle. A shorter term would strand value already
paid for or end mid-cycle.
6. Contract consolidation.
Three (3) contracts consolidate
immediately; the two (2) drone contracts roll in at their 2030
expiration as noted. Five (5) agreements become one (1) through
2034: one (1) vendor relationship, one (1) support path, and one (1)
invoice stream rather than several.
7. Basis for single-source procurement. Other manufacturers make
comparable products, but none supplies this integrated set at
comparable cost without displacing installed infrastructure and
moving 30-plus terabytes of evidence to a new custodian. The chain
of custody would have to survive for every item, and evidence
rendered unauthenticatable can be dispositive in a criminal case.
The Procurement Official has authorized the contract as a single
source.
The Department seeks to continue and consolidate a proven partnership
with Axon Enterprise, establishing fixed pricing for eight years and ensuring
two (2) cycles of critical equipment replacement.
Integration with other vendors would require extensive technical
adjustments and could disrupt ongoing operations, posing unacceptable
risks to data integrity and system reliability. Replacing Axon would not only
introduce these integration challenges but also impact operational
efficiency and continuity, as it would necessitate moving 30-plus terabytes
of evidence and risk breaking the chain of custody for critical data.

The $6.1 million additional cost associated with sourcing the same
capabilities under a separate sixth Axon contract was determined based
on vendor quotes and projected integration expenses, making the
recommended $16,064,925 the most cost-effective option for maintaining
essential services over eight (8) years. The Department recommends
approval in an amount not to exceed $16,064,925 for an eight-year term
beginning November 1, 2026.
Resolution No. 228-26 approves the contract amendment with Axon to
consolidate existing contracts into one (1) eight-year agreement effective
November 1, 2026, in an amount not to exceed $16,064,926. The
agreement fixes pricing for the term, adds nine (9) new capabilities to
systems already in service, and replaces body-worn cameras and TASERs
every five (5) years at no additional cost. The equipment will be used by
the West Palm Beach Police Department for law enforcement activities.
Funding for the first year payment of $1,061,438 is included in the FY27
Police department budget.
Fiscal Note:
Funding for year one of this contract is included in the FY27 Police
department budget.

### 7.7. Resolution No. 231-26 authorizing an amendment to the State of Florida

State Housing Initiatives Partnership Program (SHIP) Local Housing
Assistance Plan (LHAP) for program years 2024/2025 through 2026/2027
to incorporate a Manufactured Housing Assistance Strategy.
Originating Department:
Housing and Community Development
Ordinance/Resolution:
RESOLUTION NO. 231-26: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA,
APPROVING AN AMENDMENT TO THE STATE HOUSING INITIATIVES
PARTNERSHIP PROGRAM LOCAL HOUSING ASSISTANCE PLAN FOR
PROGRAM
YEARS
2024/2025
THROUGH
2026/2027
TO
INCORPORATE A MANUFACTURED HOUSING ASSISTANCE
STRATEGY IN THE PLAN; AUTHORIZING SUBMISSION OF THE
AMENDED PLAN TO THE FLORIDA HOUSING FINANCE
CORPORATION; AUTHORIZING THE MAYOR TO EXECUTE ALL
DOCUMENTS AND CERTIFICATIONS NECESSARY FOR SUBMISSION
OF THE AMENDED PLAN; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; AND
FOR OTHER PURPOSES.
Background Information:
The Florida Housing Finance Corporation (FHFC) administers the State
Housing Initiatives Partnership (SHIP) Program, which provides funding to
local governments to produce and preserve affordable housing for very
low-income, low-income, and moderate-income households. To remain
eligible to receive SHIP allocations, participating local governments must
adopt a Local Housing Assistance Plan (LHAP) establishing the strategies
and criteria used to administer their SHIP programs.
On June 10, 2024, the City Commission adopted the City’s LHAP for the
period beginning July 1, 2024, and ending June 30, 2027, through
Resolution No. 114-24.
Senate Bill 594, enacted as Chapter 2026-173, became effective July 1,
2026. The legislation amended the SHIP statutes to require each
participating local government to include strategies in its LHAP that
address the housing needs of persons who are deprived of affordable
housing due to the closure of a mobile home park and to provide SHIP
assistance to eligible mobile home owners, which must include lot rental
assistance for up to six (6) months. The legislation also repealed the
previous limitation on the percentage of SHIP funds that may be used for
manufactured housing. The manufactured housing assistance strategy is
the LHAP strategy through which the City may address these mobile home
needs.
The City’s 2024-2027 LHAP is being amended to incorporate the required
manufactured housing assistance strategy. The strategy will apply only to
the 2026/2027 SHIP allocation covered by this LHAP. Inclusion of the
strategy does not commit the City to approval of any application or award
for manufactured housing assistance. Assistance remains subject to the
availability of SHIP funds, the requirements and priorities of the applicable
LHAP, and the City’s application and approval process.
The amended LHAP is provided in Resolution No. 231-26 as Exhibit A.
Approval of Resolution No. 231-26 will authorize the amendment and
submission of the amended LHAP to FHFC for review and approval.

No fiscal impact.

the 2050 City of West Palm Beach Mobility Plan; amending the City of West

Originating Department:
Parking & Mobility Administration
Ordinance/Resolution:
ORDINANCE NO. 5182-26: AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH, FLORIDA,
ADOPTING THE 2050 CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH MOBILITY PLAN;
AMENDING THE CITY OF WEST PALM BEACH CODE OF
ORDINANCES, AT CHAPTER 86, TRAFFIC AND PARKING, ARTICLE
VII, MOBILITY FEE, SECTIONS 86-500 (MOBILITY FEES), 86-501
(DEFINITIONS), 86-502 (MOBILITY FEE IMPOSED), 86-503 (MOBILITY
FEE EXEMPTIONS), 86-504 (MOBILITY FEE SCHEDULE), 86-505
(MOBILITY FEE DETERMINATION), 86-508 (MOBILITY FEE BENEFIT
DISTRICT), 86-509 (MOBILITY FEE EXPENDITURES), 86-512
(ADMINISTRATIVE MANUAL AND SERVICE CHARGES), 86-515
(APPEALS), AND 86-516 (VESTED RIGHTS); PROVIDING FOR
CODIFICATION, CONFLICTS, SEVERABILITY AND FOR AN
EFFECTIVE DATE; AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES.
Staff Recommended Motion:
Approve Ordinance No. 5182-26.
Background Information:
In 2024, the Florida Legislature, through House Bill 479, formally defined
mobility plans and mobility fees and reaffirmed that local governments may
adopt an alternative transportation system based on a mobility plan. House
Bill 479 further provides that only one (1) transportation mitigation fee may
be collected within a municipality and that the local government issuing
building permits is responsible for collecting such fees.
In 2025, the City of West Palm Beach adopted a Downtown Mobility Fee
as the first phase of transitioning away from Palm Beach County's
transportation concurrency and road impact fee system. The proposed
ordinance represents the next step in that transition by establishing a Citywide Mobility Fee system based on the 2050 West Palm Beach Mobility
Plan and integrating the existing Downtown Mobility Fee into a
comprehensive, multimodal transportation funding framework.
The 2050 West Palm Beach Mobility Plan, dated August 2026, identifies
the mobility projects needed to accommodate projected growth through the
year 2050 and establishes a long-range strategy for enhancing the City's
multimodal transportation system. The Plan consists of six (6) elements:
Streets, Intersections, Bicycle and Pedestrian Facilities, Transit, Future
Corridors, and Mobility Implementation Projects. The mobility projects
identified in the Plan support future growth while improving accessibility,
connectivity, safety, and transportation choices for residents, businesses,
employees, and visitors.
The City of West Palm Beach Mobility Fee Technical Report, dated August
2026, documents the data, methodology, and legal analysis used to
develop the Mobility Fee. The Technical Report demonstrates that the
Mobility Fee satisfies the legally established dual rational nexus
requirements for "need" and "benefit," is roughly proportional to the impacts
of new development, and is consistent with Florida Statutes Sections
163.3164, 163.3180, 163.31801, and Chapter 380, Florida Statutes.
The proposed Mobility Fee system establishes four (4) Mobility Fee
Assessment Areas and corresponding Benefit Districts: Downtown, North,
South, and West. Mobility fees assessed on new development will be
deposited into special revenue funds and expended on mobility projects
that provide a direct benefit to the development that paid the fee. The
Mobility Fee will replace Palm Beach County road impact fees and
transportation concurrency within the City's Mobility Fee Assessment
Areas and will serve as the sole transportation mitigation fee assessed on
new development within those areas.
Mobility Fees are not a recurring tax, are not assessed on existing
residential or non-residential properties, and are not deposited into the
City's General Fund. Rather, they are a one-time assessment on new
development intended to offset the travel demand impacts associated with
growth and to fund mobility improvements identified in the 2050 West Palm
Beach Mobility Plan.
Adoption of the proposed ordinance will provide a predictable and
transparent mechanism to fund transportation improvements needed to
accommodate future growth while advancing the vision of "A West Palm
Beach where getting around is simple and seamless." The Mobility Fee
system supports a connected, multimodal transportation network and
ensures that new development contributes its proportionate share toward
the infrastructure necessary to support future residents, employees, and
visitors.

Fiscal Note:
Mobility fees that are collected will be utilized towards mobility-related
improvements specifically identified in the mobility plan.

Staff Recommended Motion:
Approve Resolution No. 208-26 for the abandonment of a portion of

Avenue, between Forest Hill Boulevard and Maddock Street, totaling
approximately 5,000 square feet of land.
This motion is based upon the application submitted, factual testimony, and
the findings that the request complies with the abandonment standards in
Section 87-217 of the City Code of Ordinances.
Background Information:
SUBJECT PROPERTY / REQUEST
The subject portion of the public right-of-way known as Winters Street is
approximately 50 feet in width by 100 feet in length and is located east of
and perpendicular to Parker Avenue, between Forest Hill Boulevard and
Maddock Street, encompassing approximately 5,000 square feet. A
property survey and legal description identifying the subject area are
provided as Exhibit A and Exhibit B.
The applicant, the School District of Palm Beach County (SDPBC), has
submitted a request to abandon a portion of Winters Street to facilitate the
consolidation of adjacent District-owned parcels for the development of an
accessory off-site surface parking lot serving Forest Hill Community High
School (FHCHS). The proposed parking facility is intended to provide
additional parking for students, faculty, staff, and visitors, while also
improving vehicular circulation and student pick-up and drop-off
operations.
BACKGROUND
Forest Hill Community High School, located west of the subject property
across Parker Avenue, originally opened in 1959 and has subsequently
expanded to serve students from throughout Palm Beach County through
its various academic and magnet programs.
Beginning in 2001, SDPBC acquired five (5) parcels in the vicinity of
Winters Street as part of its plans to modernize and redevelop FHCHS. As
part of that redevelopment, a significant portion of the school's existing onsite parking was eliminated to accommodate additional building space on
the approximately 20-acre campus. Following completion of the
redevelopment, the remaining parking supply was determined to be
insufficient to meet the school's long-term operational needs.

The School District subsequently identified the five (5) District-owned
parcels adjacent to Winters Street as a potential location for an off-site
parking facility. During the redevelopment period, the District temporarily
utilized a portion of the West Palm Beach Golf Course parking area to
supplement staff parking.
That arrangement ended following the
renovation and reopening of the golf course, further reducing the
supplemental parking available to FHCHS.
FHCHS currently serves just under 2,000 students and has approximately
272 on-site vehicular parking spaces available for faculty, staff, students,
visitors, and other campus users. The limited parking supply contributes
to congestion and circulation challenges, particularly during peak arrival
and dismissal periods.
Although roadway improvements were
subsequently completed along Parker Avenue, south of Forest Hill
Boulevard, to improve traffic flow, those improvements did not resolve the
school's ongoing parking deficiency.
2010 SPECIAL USE PERMIT
On February 4, 2010, the five (5) parcels acquired by the School District
received conditional approval from the City of West Palm Beach Zoning
Board of Appeals for a Class B Special Use Permit, under Case No. 3248,
to allow development of an accessory off-site surface parking lot serving
FHCHS. The approved proposal contemplated the use of the Districtowned property to provide additional parking and support school
operations.
Due to funding limitations, the approved parking facility was not
constructed, and the Special Use Permit subsequently expired. Because
the project did not proceed, the applicant also did not obtain approval for
the abandonment and vacation of the portion of Winters Street necessary
to consolidate the parcels and implement the proposed parking facility.
CURRENT PROPOSAL
With funding secured for the proposed off-site parking facility, the School
District is proceeding with the development of the project and is requesting
City Commission approval for the abandonment of the subject portion of
Winters Street. The proposed abandonment will facilitate the consolidation
of the subject right-of-way with the adjacent parcels, thereby creating a
unified development site necessary to accommodate construction of the
off-site parking facility.

In conjunction with the proposed abandonment of a portion of Winters
Street, the School District submitted a companion Class B Special Use
Permit (PB No. 3560) application for an 87-space accessory off-site
surface parking lot on District-owned property within the Single-Family Low
Density (SF7) zoning district. The parking facility is intended to provide
additional parking for Forest Hill Community High School (FHCHS) and
support improved vehicular circulation and student pick-up and drop-off
operations.
On September 3, 2026, the companion Class B Special Use Permit
application received unanimous approval with conditions (4-0) from the
Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA). Among the conditions of approval, the
request was specifically conditioned upon City Commission approval of the
proposed abandonment of a portion of Winters Street. Accordingly,
approval of the subject right-of-way abandonment is necessary to facilitate
the consolidation of the affected properties and allow the previously
approved 87-space off-site surface parking facility to proceed.
The subject right-of-way has limited independent development potential
and practical purpose due to the existing ownership and configuration of
the surrounding properties. The properties immediately adjoining the rightof-way to the north, south, and west are owned by the School District of
Palm Beach County (SDPBC), while the parcels proposed for the parking
facility are separated by the subject portion of Winters Street.
Abandonment of the right-of-way would allow the District to consolidate the
affected parcels and incorporate the approximately 5,000-square-foot rightof-way into the overall parking facility.
The proposed abandonment is not anticipated to adversely affect the
surrounding area or existing circulation patterns. The subject portion of
Winters Street would be incorporated into the overall parking lot design,
allowing the District to provide additional parking capacity while improving
internal vehicular circulation and school-related traffic operations.
UTILITIES & PUBLIC BENEFIT
The portion of the subject right-of-way proposed for abandonment currently
has limited function and accessibility, serving only the adjacent owner
(School District of Palm Beach County), who will benefit from the
abandonment. Therefore, there is no negative impact to the general public
by the proposed abandonment of the subject right-of-way.
Any existing utilities located within the subject right-of-way shall be
relocated as necessary in conjunction with the School District's project. All
costs associated with the relocation of utilities will be borne by the
applicant, the School District of Palm Beach County.
Because the proposed abandonment is associated with a public
educational use and is intended to provide a public benefit by improving
parking availability and traffic circulation for Forest Hill Community High
School, the City has agreed to waive the requirement for an appraisal and
payment associated with the abandonment.
In lieu of payment for the right-of-way abandonment, the applicant has
agreed to provide a pedestrian crosswalk connecting Forest Hill
Community High School to the proposed off-site parking facility along
Parker Avenue and to make improvements to the 20-foot public alley
located along the east side of the subject parking area. This improvement
will provide a designated pedestrian connection between the school
campus and the proposed parking area and will further support safe
pedestrian circulation associated with the project.
Upon approval of the right-of-way abandonment and satisfaction of all
applicable conditions, the School District of Palm Beach County will be able
to consolidate the affected parcels and proceed with development of the
proposed 87-space off-site parking facility in support of Forest Hill
Community High School.
STANDARDS
Staff found that the requested abandonment complies with the
abandonment criteria as established in Section 78-217 of the City’s Code
of Ordinances.
NOTICES
