## 4. CIVILITY AND DECORUM

      The City of West Palm Beach is committed to civility and decorum by its officials, employees
      and members of the public who attend this meeting. The City Code, Secs. 2-31(8), 2-31(18)
      and 2-31(22), provides in pertinent part:
         - Officials shall be recognized by the Chair and shall not interrupt a speaker.
         - Public comment shall be addressed to the City Commission as a whole and not to any
            individual on the dais or in the audience.
         - Displays of anger, rudeness, ridicule, impatience, lack of respect and personal attacks
            are strictly prohibited.
         - Unauthorized remarks from the audience, stamping of feet, whistles, yells and similar
            demonstrations shall not be permitted.
         - Offenders may be removed from the meeting.
         - Any person desiring to address the Commission shall file a written request with the city
            clerk prior to consideration of the matter by the Commission or prior to the public
            comment portion of a meeting. The person wishing to speak shall complete a comment
            card for each agenda item the person wishes to address, which shall include the
            person's full name, address, and the numbered agenda item. The person will not be
            recognized if the comment card is not completed.

### 7.1. Presentation recognizing the Oscar Strong Foundation for their donation

            of twelve (12) Automatic External Defibrillators (AEDs) to the City's Parks
            and Recreation Department, and installed in parks with athletic amenities
            valued at approximately $48,000.
            Originating Department:
            Parks and Recreation
            Background Information:
            The Oscar Strong Foundation, created in response to a near-tragic event
            involving six-year-old Oscar Stuebe, is making a significant impact on
            community safety. Oscar, a healthy and active child who loved baseball,
            collapsed during a game on March 10, 2024, after being struck in the
            chest by a baseball. He had no pulse. His mother immediately began
            CPR and called for an AED, but none was available at the field. Thanks to
            the rapid response of first responders and the use of an AED, Oscar’s life
            was saved.

            Oscar was diagnosed with commotio cordis, a rare but often fatal cardiac
            arrest caused by a blow to the chest. His survival was possible because
            of immediate CPR and quick defibrillation. This experience inspired his
            parents to take action.

            The Oscar Strong Foundation, led by Oscar’s mother, Sarah Stuebe, and
            co-founder Sara Mariani Mallon, was established to prevent similar
            tragedies by promoting CPR training, increasing AED accessibility, and
            advocating for proper safety equipment. In February 2025, the foundation
            installed its first AED at Phipps Park. Since then, they have partnered
            with the City of West Palm Beach Parks and Recreation Department and
            Fire Chief Diana Matty to expand this initiative.

            emergency.

City Commission - Mar 02 2026


           Eproval was established to address one of the most complex challenges
           public agencies face: managing special event and film permitting across
           multiple departments and stakeholders. Special event and film permitting
           often requires street closures, fire inspections, health permits, security
           planning, and more—all coordinated across multiple departments and
           agencies. This permitting requires more than a simple land use system
           or park reservation tool. This is where Eproval comes in—providing a
           centralized, cloud-based solution that simplifies the entire permitting
           process for applicants, reviewers, and permit managers.

           Eproval was created to handle complexity. Whether it’s a street festival,
           commercial filming, or a marathon, Eproval delivers structured workflows
           that are intuitive for applicants and efficient for reviewers. It’s designed
           specifically for permits that require multi-agency coordination, public
           safety review, and detailed operational planning.

           Special event and film applications often require additional permits for
           tents, food, alcohol, building, or pyrotechnics. Eproval is designed to
           work alongside those systems, not replace them.

           Whether your agency wants to incorporate an existing process directly
           into Eproval or provide links to an external system, our platform adapts to
           your needs. You can easily add links to departmental websites,
           downloadable forms, or step-by-step instructions right within the
           application. This flexibility keeps your internal workflows intact, while
           giving applicants a clear, streamlined experience from start to finish.

           Eproval’s software, as a service model for permit management, drives
           collaboration by providing multiple stakeholders with visibility into the
           approval process. Real-time collaboration is facilitated by Eproval’s rich
           interface that allows for alerts, messages, and real-time updates without
           the need to open other applications. This streamlined approach reduces
           the need for meetings and allows multiple approvals to happen at the
           same time. This makes the process faster, more efficient, and helps
           stakeholders stay informed.

           estimates and projections, historic and future water demands, current and
           future capital projects, revisions to the City’s water conservation efforts,
           and updates to reflect new practices and policies at the Water Treatment
           Plant.

           Element reflect the updated level of service established within the newest
           Consumptive Use Permit and the City’s fiscal year 2025 through 2030
           capital improvement projects funded or planned to be funded through the
           CIP budget process.

           Florida Statutes require the inclusion of a CIP in their Comprehensive
           Plan to show the different capital improvements that ensure that the
           different Levels of Service outlined in the Comprehensive Plan are
           maintained.
